# RePoG plugin 0.4.1: one order, a small commit

0.4.1 undoes the reply-first order of 0.3.0 and 0.4.0. It is a fix for the first
live turns on 0.4.0, where the Player received one sentence per saved turn instead
of the scene. It supersedes the persistence-order parts of `0.4.0-spec.md`.

## What went wrong

The G3 session opened on 0.4.0 and played five turns. Both saved turns ended the
same way:

1. the model thought, then called the commit;
2. after the commit it wrote only the closing line, as the writer's `hand_back`
   field told it to.

The scene itself never left the model's reasoning. The desktop app shows a short
summary of a reasoning block as plain text, so the Player saw one reported
sentence ("she says she does not know this city...") and then the closing line.

This is the same behaviour as G2's T66 and T67, the two turns where the model tried
to follow `narrate_then_commit` to the letter. There it ended with no text at all,
and the host saved the turn: `[Your previous response had no visible output. Please
continue and produce a user-visible response.]`. The 0.4.0 closing line defeated
that rescue, because a short final text passes the host's check.

So 0.4.0 did not change what the model does, it only took away the safety net:

| Evidence | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Committed turns narrated first in G2 | 0 of 160 |
| Attempts to narrate before a commit that reached the Player (G2 T66, T67; G3 T4, T5) | 0 of 4 |
| Turns with a scene summarised in the reasoning, G2 | 2 of 167 committed |
| Same, G3 on 0.4.0 | 2 of 2 committed |

The rule behind 0.4.0, that a mechanism works and an instruction does not, holds
here too: no instruction produced text before a tool call. The fix follows the host
instead of arguing with it.

## What changed

**One order.** Reads and rolls, then one commit and any due distill, then the whole
reply as the turn's last message. `narrate_then_commit` stays a valid profile value,
so older campaigns read clean, and it means this same order. The hand-back line is
gone from every instruction, and the writer's result says `reply`: send the whole
reply now.

**A tripwire.** The Stop hook counts the words the turn sent. A turn that saved a
result and sent fewer than 60 words gets an `[order]` line in the next capsule:
tell that scene in full. G2's shortest reply on a committed turn was 105 words; the
lost G3 replies were 21 and 33.

**A small commit.** G2's median commit was about 2,460 characters. The two G3 commits
were 5,238 and 6,989, partly because 0.4.0 forced `replace_section` on `## Now` and
warned on `append`, so every commit retyped the note. Now:
- `## Now` takes one appended dated line, and the writer folds the oldest into
  `## History`;
- `set_cells` changes the named columns of a table row and stamps `Revision`;
- `scene_frame.resume_anchor` alone carries the scene while it holds, since only a
  restart reads the frame;
- `resume_impact` is optional, and one roll may back several changes.

**No lookups.** A fresh session spent its first saved turns finding call shapes, one
tool call at a time: `roll_dice --help`, old receipts, `resolve_mechanic.py`'s source,
the whole 48 KB distill workflow. `mechanic_operations` was documented nowhere. The
session's first capsule, and the first after a compaction, now ends with a
`[formats]` card of about 2,200 characters with this install's launcher path, and a
full capsule shows `[mechanics]`, the ids and counts an operation addresses. Tests run
the card's shapes through the writer, so the card cannot drift from it.

## Measured

`dev/session_review/replay.py` played the same five messages through the real host on
copies of the G2 campaign at revision 175, same model and effort. P1 opens the
session and P2 to P5 are calm turns; P3 also carries a due distill. Each column is one
run, and a wait moves by ten seconds or so between runs.

| Turn | 0.4.0 wait | 0.4.0 words sent | 0.4.1 wait | 0.4.1 words sent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P1 | 29 s | 117 | 22 s | 121 |
| P2 | 77 s | 22, scene lost | 48 s | 226 |
| P3 | 110 s | 36, scene lost | 51 s | 201 |
| P4 | 62 s | 27, scene lost | 36 s | 252 |
| P5 | 10 s, nothing saved | 75 | 27 s | 164 |

| Run | 0.4.0 | 0.4.1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Committed turns with a full reply | 0 of 3 | 6 of 6 |
| Median commit size | about 3,800 characters | about 1,900 characters |
| Refused commit calls | 2 of 6 | 0 of 7 |

The 0.4.1 run went on for three more turns: an OOC money question took 5 s, and two
saved turns took 24 s and 36 s. The full check on its copy reported no errors. Before
the format card existed, P2 took 108 s and P3 82 s on the same messages.

The first version of the card caused refusals of its own: its line on
`exact_replacements` sent the model there for an inventory edit, and three refusals
in a row cost 27 s. Hence the writer's tolerance: two mutations of one owner run in
order, `ops` and `exact_replacements` may share a mutation, a replacement that
changes nothing is skipped, and `set` with a list on a list key replaces the list.
The lean wording also lost a commit once, when a turn that moved an item saved
nothing, so the capsule's order line now says `commit every changed fact`. A lean
commit can still miss an owner: in the last run a returned item reached the resume
anchor but not the inventory line.

## Left open

The reply still waits for the commit. A Stop hook can continue a turn after the reply
(`hookSpecificOutput.additionalContext`), which would hide the commit behind the
Player's reading, but the turn must then end on visible text again, and with a small
commit the gain is a few seconds. Worth a test only if waits are still a complaint.
