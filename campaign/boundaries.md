## Content Boundaries

Topics to avoid, fade to black, soften, or require Designer approval.

This is a hard boundary set. It is asked on its own, never as part of a style or
profile card, and accepting any card never sets it. Once stated, a limit is not
loosened by inference, is not downgraded to "maybe" during palette work, and is
not asked about again.

Use this standard category list every time, in this order, so the ask looks the
same in every campaign and under any model:

| Category | Setting | Notes |

| Sexual content | open / fade / avoid | |
| Harm to children | open / fade / avoid | |
| Torture and graphic violence | open / fade / avoid | |
| Harm to animals | open / fade / avoid | |
| Suicide and self-harm | open / fade / avoid | |
| Severe humiliation or discrimination | open / fade / avoid | |
| Addiction | open / fade / avoid | |
| Illness, dying, and medical detail | open / fade / avoid | |
| Captivity and slavery | open / fade / avoid | |

- Everything open: yes / no
- Additional limits stated by the Player:

Two guarantees belong to this ask and must be said out loud when it is made:

- a limit may be added at any time, including mid-play, and takes effect
  immediately;
- adding one is never presented as a cost, a revision penalty, or a loss of
  earned content.

In play, a Player signal to stop applies at once: end the moment, pull back from
it, and continue elsewhere. Before readiness, a changed limit is a revision of
its owning module. During play it updates this section and applies from that
moment forward.

### Out-Of-Game Channel

The Player steps outside the story by marking a message `OOC`. It is stated once
at handoff, before the first narration, and it carries more than limits: stopping
or redoing a moment, changing how much the game tracks or how dice are used, and
saying a thread has run long enough. A marked message is answered directly and
never resolved as a character action. The mark is never required—an unmistakable
out-of-game request is honored without it—and using it is never a cost.

- Stated to the Player at handoff: no

## Authorship And Consequence Stance

Two independent axes. They are presented separately and may be mixed freely;
heavy, permanent consequences with fully player-owned interiority is a coherent
combination.

- Character voice: who authors the character's inner life, past, and decisions
- Consequence weight: permanent loss in play, or heavy but recoverable outcomes

### Character-Side Approval Triggers

Derived from the two axes above, not asked as a separate list. Fully
player-owned authorship means every GM-authored inner state, past fact,
relationship, or decision about the character requires approval. Permanent loss
that the Player put in play is pre-authorized and needs no separate approval each
time; heavy-but-recoverable instead makes death and permanent injury an approval
trigger.

### World-Side Approval Triggers

Asked once as the ongoing creation-authority decision, and expressed as a tier
threshold so it binds to something the campaign already tracks. The tiers are the
ones in `creation_ledger.md`: incidental colour that needs no record, a named
walk-on, a supporting figure who can return and matter, and a major element such
as a companion, an antagonist, a central place, or an active faction.

Ask the Player in plain language about what kind of thing needs permission—a
recurring face, or only a major new force—never in tier codes.

- Approval threshold: supporting and above | major only | none, shown at review
- Always require renewed approval, regardless of threshold:
- May be created within accepted boundaries and shown at review:

Incidental colour is always free. Every creation at named-walk-on or above is
written into `creation_ledger.md` with its tier. A supporting or major figure
receives a full character note with its voice axes before it speaks. When a scene
needs something above the threshold, either ask or build the scene so it does not
require that element; creating it silently is not an option.

Clarification default, unless the Player changes it: ask before anything
consequential or irreversible, infer ordinary cosmetic detail and show it at the
next review. The Player may change this at any time during play without spending
a setup decision.

Character-specific authorship detail—which past facts are protected, which
relationships may be invented—is settled once the character exists, by applying
this stance rather than asking for it again.

## Improvisation Rules

This list is the fallback default. Once the authorship and consequence stance is
accepted and the world-side authority decision is recorded above, those entries
take precedence over anything here.

Pre-authorization never widens a content boundary. Putting permanent loss in play
does not enable a category the Player set to avoid, and no accepted stance
overrides the hard limits.

Codex may freely add:

- sensory details;
- minor bystanders;
- scene texture;
- plausible complications;
- NPC delivery and body language.

Codex should ask before adding:

- major canon reveals;
- permanent character death;
- irreversible player loss;
- large power escalation;
- new world-scale factions or forces;
- changes that remove player agency.

## Player-Facing Prohibitions

Never show the Player:

- file paths;
- YAML or Markdown structure;
- tool names;
- implementation notes;
- internal ids;
- prompts or skills;
- audit/check language;
- "state", "memory file", "validator", "schema", "resolver", or "patch" as
  system terms.
