# Forgotten Realms Campaign Frames

## Frame Contract

These are original, selectable campaign frameworks. They are not published Forgotten Realms plots, predetermined stories, or active state. Each frame provides a causal pressure, actors with motives, neutral action space, and an expansion path. Session 0 must still establish player ties, boundaries, tone, exact locale, and accepted canon.

Player-facing summaries are safe to present. Sections marked `GM-only proposal` must not be exposed before their reveal conditions are earned.

## Default Frame

### `fr-frame-silent-bell` — The Bell The Forest Silenced

- Region: `fr-region-dalelands`
- Status: recommended default
- Player-facing promise: A road linking two dale communities has gone quiet. Reopen it, learn why each side tells a different story, and decide what the old forest is allowed to remember.
- Initial scale: one home settlement, one neighboring settlement, one trade road, and the bordering edge of Cormanthor
- Suggested tier: local and early-career heroes
- Tone: classic heroic fantasy, community pressure, forest mystery, and faction intrigue
- Character fit: local protector, traveler, scholar, trader, ranger, priest, faction contact, elf-linked character, or person tied to a missing caravan

#### Visible Situation

- Caravans and messengers have stopped arriving.
- Each settlement blames a different threat.
- Food, medicine, tools, and trust are beginning to run short.
- Forest wardens have closed a route without explaining enough to satisfy local leaders.

#### Active Cause-And-Effect Chain

1. Salvagers remove components from an old protective network.
2. The damaged network distorts routes and drives inhabitants from part of the forest.
3. Displaced groups raid or seize supplies along the road.
4. Trade failures increase fear and make predatory contracts attractive.
5. Outside buyers gain land and influence as residents become desperate.

#### Actors And Motives

- Local council: restore trade before the community fractures
- Neighboring dale delegation: prove it did not cause the disappearances
- Elf wardens: keep people away until the damaged protection can be understood
- Displaced goblinoid community: obtain food and safe territory after losing its home
- Zhentarim-linked buyers: deepen the crisis enough to acquire land, routes, and dependency
- Salvager organizer: recover one final component needed to clear a personal debt

#### GM-Only Proposal

- Hidden truth id: `fr-frame-dale-secret-001`
- Truth: no single group created the crisis; removal of old ward components caused cascading displacement, and the profitable response is now prolonging it
- Reveal requirements: physical evidence at two disruption sites plus testimony from actors with opposing interests
- Safe foreshadowing: routes behaving inconsistently, scavenged fittings, frightened rather than conquering raiders, unusually prepared buyers
- Forbidden early wording: do not announce that the wards or Zhentarim-linked buyers are the complete cause

#### Neutral Action Space

Players may negotiate access, escort relief, investigate sites, expose contracts, protect or relocate displaced people, restore or redesign the ward, side with one settlement, recruit faction aid, or reject every offered institutional solution.

#### Inaction Trajectory

1. Shortage: prices rise, patrols harden, and outsiders offer protection.
2. Fracture: each settlement closes access and displaced groups consolidate around the road.
3. Capture: the route reopens under coercive private control while the damaged forest becomes a larger magical hazard.

#### PC Tie Prompts

- Who was on the last missing caravan?
- Which settlement do you need to believe you?
- What did your family gain from the road?
- Which faction once helped you at a price?
- What part of the forest do you consider home?

#### Boundaries And Adaptation

- Avoid species-wide villainy; give displaced people internal disagreement and agency.
- Change the faction buyer if Zhentarim use is not desired.
- Reduce magical complexity by making the ward a physical route system with a smaller supernatural effect.
- Expand toward Myth Drannor, wider Dalelands politics, Cormyr, Sembia, or cross-regional faction conflict only after local consequences land.

## Urban Frame

### `fr-frame-ash-ledger` — The Ledger Of Ash

- Region: `fr-region-baldurs-gate`
- Player-facing promise: After a harbor fire, one missing ledger becomes more valuable than the cargo that burned. Find it before the city's protectors, criminals, and creditors decide who must disappear with it.
- Initial scale: one harbor neighborhood, one gate route, one enforcement office, and one elite commercial circle
- Suggested tier: street-level investigators and operators
- Tone: urban intrigue, crime, class pressure, and infernal temptation
- Character fit: investigator, dockworker, criminal, merchant, Flaming Fist contact, Guild debtor, noble dissident, cult survivor, or outsider with a forged identity

#### Visible Situation

- A warehouse fire destroyed valuable cargo and displaced workers.
- Officials focus on a missing account book rather than the victims.
- Witnesses are being paid, arrested, or frightened into silence.
- Both the Guild and a respectable trading house claim the ledger contains stolen property records.

#### Active Cause-And-Effect Chain

1. A commercial circle finances influence through concealed supernatural contracts.
2. The ledger maps obligations linking officials, enforcement, and vulnerable businesses.
3. A clerk tries to extract the evidence during a staged fire.
4. Competing actors hunt the ledger, endangering witnesses and neighborhoods.
5. Public exposure could free debtors, trigger civic panic, or transfer power to a rival exploiter.

#### Actors And Motives

- Displaced workers: aid, safety, and accountability
- Honest Flaming Fist officer: secure admissible evidence before corrupt superiors intervene
- Guild intermediary: keep the illicit economy predictable and prevent a more dangerous faction from taking over
- Trading-house heir: destroy the ledger to preserve family and civic standing
- Infernal financier's agent: recover enforceable names and obligations
- Missing clerk: survive long enough to trade the truth for protection

#### GM-Only Proposal

- Hidden truth id: `fr-frame-gate-secret-001`
- Truth: the ledger records political obligations disguised as ordinary commercial debt; destroying it protects some victims while preserving the system
- Reveal requirements: compare surviving warehouse records, a debtor's testimony, and the contract notation key
- Safe foreshadowing: officials asking about page numbers, identical marks on unrelated contracts, victims afraid of debt rather than arson charges
- Forbidden early wording: do not identify the infernal mechanism or final moral tradeoff during Session 0

#### Neutral Action Space

Players may locate the clerk, copy or alter the ledger, protect witnesses, bargain with the Guild, work through law, expose selected names, break contracts, sell the evidence, or use it to build a new coalition.

#### Inaction Trajectory

1. Sweep: vulnerable witnesses are arrested or bought.
2. Consolidation: one faction acquires the ledger and absorbs rival debts.
3. Normalization: the fire is blamed on disposable culprits while supernatural finance becomes embedded in city policy.

#### PC Tie Prompts

- Whose livelihood was in the warehouse?
- Which authority once ignored your evidence?
- What debt could the ledger erase?
- Who believes you owe them loyalty?
- Which neighborhood will hide you—and what does it expect in return?

#### Boundaries And Adaptation

- Agree on corruption, coercion, fire, poverty, and infernal-horror limits.
- Do not assume any *Baldur's Gate 3* ending.
- Replace infernal finance with mundane blackmail for lower supernatural intensity.
- Expand to city government, wider Sword Coast trade, or planar stakes only after the neighborhood responds.

## High-Magic Political Frame

### `fr-frame-four-courts-well` — The Well Of Four Courts

- Region: `fr-region-calimshan`
- Player-facing promise: A well sustaining caravans and three communities begins producing four impossible kinds of water. Decide who has a right to repair it before ancient obligations turn scarcity into war.
- Initial scale: one city district or town, two nearby communities, one caravan route, and the contested well
- Suggested tier: locally trusted troubleshooters, travelers, or negotiators
- Tone: high magic, resource politics, contracts, travel, and elemental wonder
- Character fit: local official, caravan guard, trader, scholar, genie-sworn paladin, elemental caster, community advocate, or heir to an old obligation

#### Visible Situation

- The well alternates among healing mist, scalding water, mineral-heavy sludge, and water that rises into the air.
- Competing authorities issue incompatible access rules.
- Caravans reroute, prices rise, and neighboring communities accuse one another of sabotage.
- Genie representatives offer spectacular but mutually exclusive solutions.

#### Active Cause-And-Effect Chain

1. An old resource accord relied on four elemental witnesses and one mortal steward.
2. The steward's legal identity disappeared through political erasure and lost records.
3. The accord cannot resolve authority and cycles among incompatible provisions.
4. Scarcity makes unilateral control profitable.
5. Mortal and genie claimants use the malfunction to renegotiate regional power.

#### Actors And Motives

- Water council: preserve access and prevent panic
- Caravan coalition: restore predictable supply quickly
- Nearby mobile community: defend seasonal rights ignored by city records
- Calimport investor: finance a repair in exchange for ownership
- Four court envoys: defend elemental precedent and gain influence
- Local archivist: restore the erased steward's history and descendants

#### GM-Only Proposal

- Hidden truth id: `fr-frame-calim-secret-001`
- Truth: the missing party was a mortal office shared by communities, not an individual bloodline; claimants are misreading the accord for political advantage
- Reveal requirements: compare oral history, physical inscriptions, and records written by two rival institutions
- Safe foreshadowing: plural titles translated as singular, repeated community symbols, court envoys avoiding the question of mortal governance
- Forbidden early wording: do not disclose the true nature of the fifth party

#### Neutral Action Space

Players may enforce rationing, escort water, investigate records, seek community testimony, negotiate with courts, accept investment, repair the accord, replace it, destroy the well's magic, or create a new public institution.

#### Inaction Trajectory

1. Scarcity: hoarding and route changes hurt the least protected communities.
2. Seizure: a claimant militarizes access with outside support.
3. Elemental breach: the unresolved accord opens the site to dangerous planar overflow.

#### PC Tie Prompts

- Whose livelihood depends on this route?
- Which promise did your family inherit without understanding?
- Which community taught you how to survive here?
- What kind of magical patronage do you distrust?
- What would you sacrifice to keep water public?

#### Boundaries And Adaptation

- Center local agency and institutions; genies are participants, not the only meaningful powers.
- Avoid generalized exoticism and research the selected locale before finalization.
- Reduce magic by turning the four states into alchemical contamination and legal sabotage.
- Expand to Calimport, desert routes, rival courts, or elemental planes after resolving local access.

## Survival Frame

### `fr-frame-last-warm-road` — The Last Warm Road

- Region: `fr-region-icewind-dale`
- Player-facing promise: The final dependable supply road begins freezing weeks too early. Keep communities alive, find what changed beneath the ice, and choose whose survival plan becomes the Dale's future.
- Initial scale: two towns, one supply route, one refuge, and a nearby stretch of tundra
- Suggested tier: locally capable but resource-limited heroes
- Tone: survival pressure, rescue, community politics, and buried magic
- Character fit: guide, fisher, healer, town advocate, Reghed or dwarven character, Winter Walker, hunter, scholar, or supply organizer

#### Visible Situation

- Ice repeatedly seals a road shortly after crews clear it.
- A missing rescue party carried equipment needed for the next supply run.
- Town leaders disagree over rationing and evacuation.
- A displaced traveling community warns that the cold is moving against the wind.

#### Active Cause-And-Effect Chain

1. An expedition activates part of a buried climate-control complex while attempting a rescue.
2. The incomplete activation redirects cold along old infrastructure corridors.
3. The road freezes, migration routes shift, and predators follow displaced prey.
4. Towns compete for shrinking supplies and blame outsiders.
5. A second activation could stabilize one area by sacrificing another.

#### Actors And Motives

- Town speakers: protect constituents and retain legitimacy
- Supply organizer: keep the road open at almost any cost
- Reghed representatives: preserve a migration path and recover missing people
- Dwarven engineers: understand the buried structure before anyone destroys it
- Expedition sponsor: conceal responsibility and recover valuable research
- Rescue survivors: stop others from repeating their mistake

#### GM-Only Proposal

- Hidden truth id: `fr-frame-ice-secret-001`
- Truth: the complex was activated during a failed rescue rather than for conquest, and every simple shutdown plan transfers danger elsewhere
- Reveal requirements: survivor account, route-temperature pattern, and access to one control site
- Safe foreshadowing: geometric frost, warm zones appearing elsewhere, rescue marks near machinery, predators moving rather than multiplying
- Forbidden early wording: do not reveal the expedition's role or zero-sum control problem

#### Neutral Action Space

Players may rescue survivors, escort supplies, mediate rationing, open an alternate route, study or sabotage the system, relocate communities, bargain for expertise, expose the sponsor, or redesign the activation plan.

#### Inaction Trajectory

1. Rationing: prices, illness, and political blame intensify.
2. Fragmentation: towns close borders while migration and wildlife patterns collapse.
3. Abandonment: one population survives by directing lethal conditions toward another.

#### PC Tie Prompts

- Who depends on the next supply run?
- What did an earlier winter take from you?
- Which community distrusts you, and why?
- Who taught you a route that maps do not show?
- What resource have you promised never to hoard?

#### Boundaries And Adaptation

- Agree on exposure, starvation, injury, isolation, and body-horror limits.
- Use survival choices, not repetitive arithmetic, unless the group requests detailed logistics.
- Ensure local and mobile communities possess expertise and political agency.
- Expand toward wider Ten-Towns, glacier, sea ice, mountains, or Underdark only after local consequences.

## Seafaring And Fey Frame

### `fr-frame-salted-moonwell` — Salt In The Moonwell

- Region: `fr-region-moonshae`
- Player-facing promise: A sacred freshwater source turns salty, and the surrounding land begins taking the shape of the sea. Cross island boundaries and old promises before every community chooses a different enemy.
- Initial scale: one island community, one sacred site, one sea lane, and one neighboring shore or undersea contact
- Suggested tier: community-bound travelers and mediators
- Tone: seafaring, fey mystery, ecological change, and oath-driven politics
- Character fit: sailor, druid, moon bard, fisher, island envoy, Norlander or Ffolk character, fey-touched traveler, oath keeper, or undersea diplomat

#### Visible Situation

- Freshwater becomes brackish while crops and animals change.
- A recent storm moved old stones near the coast.
- Druids close the sacred site, fishers demand access, and sailors report lights beneath the water.
- Rival communities interpret the event as curse, attack, or broken ritual.

#### Active Cause-And-Effect Chain

1. A storm displaces a boundary marker linking land, fey, and undersea claims.
2. Each realm begins enforcing its own ecological conditions across the overlap.
3. Livelihoods fail and communities accuse historic rivals.
4. Opportunists use fear to justify raids or permanent exclusion.
5. The longer the boundary remains unresolved, the harder it becomes to separate the affected environments without loss.

#### Actors And Motives

- Island council: restore water and prevent panic
- Moonshae druids: protect the sacred site and avoid a reckless magical repair
- Fishing families: retain access and livelihood
- Norlander crew: recover people or property lost in the storm and resist collective blame
- Fey representative: restore a promise whose meaning mortals have forgotten
- Undersea envoys: defend a settlement now affected by runoff and shifted magic

#### GM-Only Proposal

- Hidden truth id: `fr-frame-moon-secret-001`
- Truth: no faction launched an attack; a physical boundary change made several valid old promises contradict one another
- Reveal requirements: inspect both sides of the boundary, recover the original ritual account, and hear testimony from mortal and nonmortal claimants
- Safe foreshadowing: symmetrical damage, old markers in new places, every group suffering a different effect, ritual language about borders rather than ownership
- Forbidden early wording: do not announce accidental boundary overlap as the answer

#### Neutral Action Space

Players may secure water, escort sailors, investigate underwater, negotiate temporary access, restore the old boundary, create a new shared rite, favor one environment, expose opportunists, or move the sacred site at a cost.

#### Inaction Trajectory

1. Blame: access closes and retaliatory raids begin.
2. Transformation: farmland, shore, and undersea habitat overwrite one another.
3. War of remedies: each faction attempts a unilateral ritual that could permanently erase another claimant's home.

#### PC Tie Prompts

- Which shore do you call home?
- What promise did your community stop honoring?
- Who vanished during the storm?
- Which livelihood would your preferred solution harm?
- What did the fey once give your family?

#### Boundaries And Adaptation

- Do not reduce Norlanders to raiders or Ffolk to victims.
- Give fey consistent values and limits rather than random behavior.
- Agree on transformation, drowning, ecological loss, and coercive bargain boundaries.
- Expand to the archipelago, Feywild, Sword Coast, or deep ocean only after local restitution or rupture.
