# Forgotten Realms Regions

## Use Contract

Region cards describe reusable campaign affordances. They are neither a claim that the package fully models Faerûn nor accepted campaign state. Select one opening region, prune it to a playable local scale, and research any unresolved canon that affects that scale.

The five primary cards below follow the regions given deep campaign treatment in the 2025 *Adventures in Faerûn* presentation. Supplementary cards use other official fifth-edition entry points and require targeted source confirmation before a strict-canon lock.

## Primary 2025 Campaign Regions

### `fr-region-dalelands` — Dalelands

- Source status: primary 2025 campaign region
- Campaign promise: ordinary communities confront forest danger, political encroachment, and the living legacy of elven high magic
- First-glance identity: fertile dales, trade roads, independent settlements, old alliances, Cormanthor, and the ruins of Myth Drannor
- Routine life: farming, craft, local councils, inns, markets, woodland work, patrols, and inter-dale trade
- Stable institutions: individual dale leadership, the Dales Council as a cooperative forum, community defense, and local custom
- Active pressures: vulnerable roads, opportunistic outside powers, forest threats, disputed ruins, and communities that resist distant control
- Likely actors: dalefolk, elves of Cormanthor, Zhentarim interests, Sembian interests, travelers, salvagers, and local defenders
- Travel model: road and forest choices matter; a safe route can become a political asset
- Affordances: local heroism, diplomacy, monster ecology, mystery, escort, defense, lost lore, faction play, and megadungeon expansion
- Dangers: flattening every threat into raiders; treating ruins as ownerless treasure; making rural people passive
- Player-known account: the dales value independence, old forest alliances matter, and nearby ruins draw both heroes and exploiters
- GM caution: select only the active dale, one route network, and one historical layer at opening scale
- Character fits: local protector, ranger, wandering priest, Banneret, Harper contact, scholar, trader, elf-linked character, or reformed faction operative
- Expansion boundary: first neighboring dales and Cormanthor; later Cormyr, Sembia, Moonsea routes, or wider Faerûn
- Source claims: `claim-dalelands-2025`

### `fr-region-baldurs-gate` — Baldur's Gate

- Source status: primary 2025 campaign region
- Campaign promise: survive a wealthy port where law, class, organized crime, cult influence, and trade are inseparable
- First-glance identity: a hard-edged city at the Chionthar, socially and physically divided among upper, lower, and outer districts
- Routine life: dock work, caravan traffic, markets, tolls, patronage, private security, cramped housing, and rumor exchange
- Stable institutions: Council of Four, Parliament of Peers, Flaming Fist, merchant interests, and the Guild's criminal economy
- Active pressures: elite privilege, coercive enforcement, corruption, smuggling, cult cells, and infernal bargains
- Likely actors: civic officials, patriars, Flaming Fist officers, Guild crews, laborers, outsiders, Dead Three cultists, and devil-aligned conspirators
- Travel model: gates, bridges, harbor access, district boundaries, and legal status shape movement
- Affordances: investigation, heist, courtroom or council pressure, street alliances, faction infiltration, trade disputes, and moral compromise
- Dangers: assuming a *Baldur's Gate 3* ending; overwhelming new players with cameos; portraying every institution as uniformly corrupt
- Player-known account: money and connections matter, enforcement can be harsh, and respectable power often overlaps hidden power
- GM caution: define one district, one institution, and one street-level pressure before opening the whole city
- Character fits: investigator, criminal survivor, guild debtor, mercenary, noble dissident, cult escapee, merchant, or urban priest
- Expansion boundary: city districts, river and Coast Way, then wider Sword Coast politics or planar consequences
- Source claims: `claim-baldurs-gate-2025`

### `fr-region-calimshan` — Calimshan

- Source status: primary 2025 campaign region
- Campaign promise: negotiate trade, water, law, and ancient elemental obligations in a land where mortal and genie powers compete
- First-glance identity: deserts, fertile coasts, caravan routes, Calimport, magical commerce, and four kinds of genie court
- Routine life: port and market work, craft, patronage, caravans, oasis governance, steppe travel, local scholarship, and magical services in major centers
- Stable institutions: mortal government, trade and craft interests, local councils, nomadic leadership, and genie courts with old claims
- Active pressures: water rights, commercial rivalry, inherited bargains, elemental patronage, contested routes, and unequal magical power
- Likely actors: Calimport magnates, craftspeople, officials, Kochar representatives, caravaneers, dao, djinn, efreet, marids, and their mortal agents
- Travel model: heat, water, route knowledge, oasis agreements, and aerial or elemental routes create leverage
- Affordances: diplomacy, contract interpretation, high magic, trade, exploration, elemental adventure, and political reform
- Dangers: reducing the region to an exotic bazaar; giving all agency to genies; treating local people as a single culture
- Player-known account: every bargain has context, water and routes carry political weight, and magical sophistication does not erase inequality
- GM caution: research local presentation and establish mortal institutions before adding spectacular genie courts
- Character fits: genie-sworn paladin, merchant, local reformer, caravan guard, scholar, elemental caster, diplomat, or indebted heir
- Expansion boundary: one city-route-oasis triangle, then rival courts, coast, desert ruins, and broader trade networks
- Source claims: `claim-calimshan-2025`

### `fr-region-icewind-dale` — Icewind Dale

- Source status: primary 2025 campaign region
- Campaign promise: protect fragile communities where cold, isolation, scarcity, and supernatural danger make every commitment costly
- First-glance identity: Ten-Towns, tundra, glaciers, mountains, moving sea ice, dwarven holdings, Reghed communities, giants, and predators
- Routine life: fishing, hunting, mining, trade, repair, guiding, food storage, weather watching, and mutual survival work
- Stable institutions: individual town governments, local trade, kin and tribal structures, guides, craft networks, and improvised cooperation
- Active pressures: early winter, route failure, resource disputes, isolation, awakened ruins, monsters, and political fragmentation
- Likely actors: Ten-Towns residents, Reghed tribes, dwarves, goliaths, giants, traders, guides, scholars, and opportunists
- Travel model: weather, shelter, daylight, supplies, local knowledge, and rescue obligations matter
- Affordances: survival, rescue, horror, expedition, community politics, buried magic, monster hunting, and hard moral choices
- Dangers: turning logistics into repetitive bookkeeping; treating local or nomadic people as helpless or primitive; using cold only as damage
- Player-known account: cooperation keeps people alive, every trip has a cost, and the wilderness preserves dangerous history
- GM caution: agree on survival intensity and body-horror limits before lock
- Character fits: Winter Walker, guide, town advocate, Reghed or dwarven character, healer, hunter, exile, scholar, or supply organizer
- Expansion boundary: one town cluster and route, then wider tundra, glacier, sea ice, Spine of the World, or Underdark
- Source claims: `claim-icewind-dale-2025`

### `fr-region-moonshae` — Moonshae Isles

- Source status: primary 2025 campaign region
- Campaign promise: cross dangerous seas and negotiate the balance among island communities, sacred nature, fey influence, and maritime powers
- First-glance identity: misted islands, villages, longships, old barrows, moonwells, fey crossings, druids, storms, and contested coasts
- Routine life: fishing, farming, sailing, repair, trade, seasonal gatherings, sacred observance, and navigation
- Stable institutions: Ffolk communities, Norlander settlements and crews, druidic guardians, island rulers, and fey relationships
- Active pressures: ecological corruption, broken oaths, raiding, maritime rivalry, supernatural contagion, and territorial claims above and below the sea
- Likely actors: Ffolk, Norlanders, druids, fey courts and kin, sailors, traders, coastal raiders, and undersea powers
- Travel model: tides, weather, vessels, safe harbors, seasonal routes, and fey crossings determine access
- Affordances: seafaring, swashbuckling, mediation, ritual, exploration, community defense, fey bargains, and environmental mystery
- Dangers: portraying Norlanders only as raiders; portraying Ffolk only as pastoral victims; making fey behavior random instead of value-driven
- Player-known account: sea and land are linked, old promises protect communities, and natural imbalance has social consequences
- GM caution: define the party's vessel access and the difference between mortal and fey obligations
- Character fits: sailor, moon bard, druid, island envoy, privateer, fey-touched character, fisher, oath keeper, or undersea diplomat
- Expansion boundary: one island group and sea lane, then wider archipelago, Sword Coast, Feywild, or deep ocean
- Source claims: `claim-moonshae-2025`

## Supplementary Entry Regions

### `fr-region-phandalin` — Phandalin And The Nearby Sword Coast

- Source status: official fifth-edition onboarding area; not one of the five 2025 deep-dive regions
- Campaign promise: learn the world through a frontier settlement, nearby roads, mines, ruins, and competing local interests
- Strength: smallest lore burden and clearest beginner ramp
- Pressure palette: road safety, missing travelers, local power gaps, resource claims, and old sites
- Caution: do not reproduce a published adventure's plot or assume its outcome without selection
- Character fits: nearly any beginning adventurer
- Expansion boundary: settlement, road network, nearby coast and cities
- Source claims: `claim-phandalin-onboarding`

### `fr-region-waterdeep` — Waterdeep

- Source status: official fifth-edition urban entry area
- Campaign promise: pursue status, secrets, crime, commerce, and civic influence in a large cosmopolitan city
- Strength: broad character support and social play
- Pressure palette: guilds, noble interests, law, hidden wealth, neighborhood loyalties, and faction competition
- Caution: high lore and named-NPC load; begin with one ward and one institution
- Character fits: social characters, investigators, criminals, nobles, guild members, scholars, and faction agents
- Expansion boundary: ward, city, Sword Coast, Undermountain, or wider alliance politics
- Source claims: `claim-waterdeep-entry`

### `fr-region-underdark` — Underdark

- Source status: official setting corner; targeted research required before lock
- Campaign promise: navigate isolated environments and competing subterranean societies where light, route knowledge, food, and trust matter
- Strength: distinctive exploration and political survival
- Pressure palette: vertical geography, scarce safe routes, predation, migration, ancient sites, and social misunderstanding
- Caution: reject species-wide moral essentialism and surface-centric assumptions
- Character fits: exile, scout, trader, scholar, escaped captive, local citizen, or emissary
- Expansion boundary: one route and settlement network before broader depths
- Source claims: `claim-fr-entry-corners`

### `fr-region-chult` — Chult

- Source status: official setting corner; targeted cultural and current-canon research required before lock
- Campaign promise: undertake ecological, political, and historical exploration in a region with living local societies and dangerous environments
- Strength: expedition play and layered discovery
- Pressure palette: weather, disease policy, supply chains, local sovereignty, competing expeditions, ruins, and powerful creatures
- Caution: never frame the region as empty land awaiting outside discovery; local people retain expertise and agency
- Character fits: local guide, scholar, healer, merchant, naturalist, diplomat, sailor, or expedition member
- Expansion boundary: one community and route before continental-scale exploration
- Source claims: `claim-fr-entry-corners`

### `fr-region-cormyr` — Cormyr

- Source status: durable setting region; targeted 2025 confirmation required before strict lock
- Campaign promise: balance service, law, honor, court politics, border defense, and personal conscience in a strong realm
- Strength: clear institutions for knightly and political characters
- Pressure palette: competing duties, noble interests, military command, border threats, succession concerns, and public legitimacy
- Caution: verify current officeholders and avoid making state authority automatically righteous
- Character fits: Banneret, soldier, courtier, dissident noble, investigator, diplomat, or border resident
- Expansion boundary: one command, settlement, or court network before the whole kingdom
- Source claims: `claim-banneret-context`

## Default Selection

`fr-region-dalelands` is the recommended default because it supports broad character concepts, low-to-moderate lore load, local stakes, faction pressure, travel, diplomacy, and natural escalation into iconic Forgotten Realms history.

`fr-region-phandalin` remains the recommended alternative when the group prioritizes the most familiar beginner structure over a fresher campaign identity.
