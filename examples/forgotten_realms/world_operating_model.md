# Forgotten Realms World Operating Model

## Campaign-Neutral Promise

Faerûn supports local beginnings that can grow into legend. Ancient power remains embedded in roads, ruins, bloodlines, faiths, laws, and landscapes. Modern communities must live with that inheritance while states, factions, cults, monsters, and supernatural powers pursue incompatible futures.

## Active Cause-And-Effect Chain

1. Layered history leaves valuable, dangerous, and politically meaningful remnants.
2. Unequal access to safety, magic, knowledge, and trade gives institutions reasons to control those remnants.
3. Competing actors place pressure on ordinary communities through law, secrecy, commerce, faith, violence, and promises.
4. Local disruptions reveal deeper historical or factional causes.
5. Player intervention redistributes information, legitimacy, resources, and risk.
6. Surviving actors adapt, making consequences visible beyond the solved encounter.

## Beneficiaries And Costs

- Beneficiaries of stability: established rulers, trade networks, temples, protected communities, and institutions whose authority is recognized
- Beneficiaries of instability: opportunistic factions, smugglers, cults, expansionist powers, scavengers, and actors excluded from the present order
- Commonly displaced costs: frontier settlements, travelers, marginalized communities, conscripts, debtors, and people living near old magic
- Player leverage: reveal hidden costs, redirect resources, legitimize an excluded claimant, break an exploitative arrangement, or replace one unstable bargain with another

## Durable World Truths

### `fr-truth-001` — Faerûn Is A Scope, Not A Single Culture

- Truth: The default package concerns Faerûn on Toril; Faerûn contains many governments, cultures, climates, faith practices, and levels of magical access.
- Table impact: Region cards override generic assumptions about law, daily life, travel, and authority.
- Open question: Which locale defines the opening scale?
- Source claims: `claim-fr-scope`, `claim-five-regions`

### `fr-truth-002` — History Remains Material

- Truth: Successive civilizations have left ruins, wards, roads, claims, relics, and collective memories that still affect the present.
- Table impact: A ruin should matter to a living actor; lore changes decisions rather than serving only as exposition.
- Open question: Which one or two historical layers are active in the opening region?
- Source claims: `claim-fr-identity`, `claim-dalelands-2025`

### `fr-truth-003` — The Weave Makes Magic Systemic But Uneven

- Truth: The Weave is central to how much Faerûnian magic is understood and accessed, but magical expertise and services are distributed unevenly.
- Table impact: Use regional access bands—scarce, uncommon, established, or abundant—instead of a universal spellcaster percentage.
- Open question: What can ordinary people reliably obtain in the selected locale?
- Source claims: `claim-weave`, `claim-heroes-contents`

### `fr-truth-004` — Magical Disruption Has Causes

- Truth: Dead magic, wild magic, broken wards, and unstable sites can result from historical disaster, damaged infrastructure, or deliberate action.
- Table impact: A magical anomaly must have a source, beneficiaries, victims, and consequences if ignored.
- Open question: Is unstable magic part of the selected frame?
- Source claims: `claim-weave`

### `fr-truth-005` — Gods Are Real, Institutions Are Mortal

- Truth: Divine powers, omens, clerics, sacred sites, and afterlife beliefs have objective weight, while temples and worshipers remain political and fallible.
- Table impact: Faith can grant real power without making every religious authority correct.
- Open question: Which faiths shape the opening community?
- Source claims: `claim-deities-2025`

### `fr-truth-006` — Divine Action Usually Requires Mortal Agency

- Truth: Gods do not routinely solve local crises directly; their influence is commonly mediated through people, signs, relics, places, and competing interpretations.
- Table impact: Divine stakes produce choices rather than automatic answers.
- Open question: How explicit should omens and divine communication be?
- Source claims: `claim-deities-2025`

### `fr-truth-007` — Political Authority Is Regional

- Truth: Faerûn has no single sovereign authority. Law may come from a city council, crown, local compact, mercenary force, temple, clan, or informal power.
- Table impact: Jurisdiction, punishment, property, and legitimacy must be defined at the campaign scale.
- Open question: Who can credibly make and enforce law in the starting place?
- Source claims: `claim-five-regions`

### `fr-truth-008` — Factions Act Beyond The Party's Sight

- Truth: Cross-regional and local factions pursue goals even when players do not engage them.
- Table impact: Every active faction needs a desire, method, limit, visible trace, and inaction clock.
- Open question: Which two or three factions are active at opening scale?
- Source claims: `claim-factions-2025`

### `fr-truth-009` — Travel Has Consequences

- Truth: Distance, weather, terrain, tolls, borders, monsters, and information delay make travel meaningful.
- Table impact: Skip uneventful repetition, but preserve choices about route, time, company, exposure, and resources.
- Open question: Which travel pressure best supports the selected tone?
- Source claims: `claim-region-contrast`

### `fr-truth-010` — Communication Is Unequal

- Truth: Couriers, caravans, ships, rumor, and local networks remain important; magical communication exists but is not universal public infrastructure.
- Table impact: Information can arrive late, altered, censored, or monopolized.
- Open question: Who controls reliable news in the opening region?
- Source claims: `claim-fr-identity`

### `fr-truth-011` — Peoples Are Not Moral Monoliths

- Truth: Species, ancestry, homeland, faith, and faction may shape experience but do not determine a person's morality or personality.
- Table impact: Communities and adversaries need material motives, internal differences, and individual agency.
- Open question: Which identities require additional cultural research for the selected locale?
- Source claims: `claim-heroes-character-context`

### `fr-truth-012` — Monsters Belong To A World

- Truth: A creature can be a predator, citizen, claimant, weapon, survivor, sacred being, or political actor rather than a random combat delivery system.
- Table impact: Significant encounters should connect to ecology, history, faction action, or local consequence.
- Open question: Which threats can be negotiated with, and who benefits from calling them monsters?
- Source claims: `claim-region-contrast`

### `fr-truth-013` — Adventurers Are Known But Not Automatically Trusted

- Truth: Professional or self-appointed adventurers are recognizable, yet their authority and reputation vary by place.
- Table impact: Access and trust follow deeds, ties, sponsorship, and renown rather than protagonist status.
- Open question: Why does the opening community tolerate or need this party?
- Source claims: `claim-heroes-contents`

### `fr-truth-014` — Death Matters Despite Powerful Magic

- Truth: Restoration and resurrection may be possible, but access, cost, expertise, timing, body, and soul conditions keep death socially serious.
- Table impact: Do not treat resurrection as universal insurance or remove consequences without an accepted tone choice.
- Open question: How available should restorative magic be at opening scale?
- Source claims: `claim-rules-55`

### `fr-truth-015` — Campaign History Outranks Later Franchise Changes

- Truth: After materialization, accepted player-caused history is authoritative for that campaign.
- Table impact: Package updates are optional migrations, never retroactive rewrites.
- Open question: What canon-flexibility mode did Session 0 accept?
- Source claims: `claim-canon-editions`

## Historical Spine

Use history as playable pressure, not a mandatory lecture.

| Layer | Durable present-day effect | Confidence posture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ancient draconic, elven, dwarven, and other early powers | Competing claims, ruins, old roads, dangerous inheritances | Use regional sources before specifics |
| Elven realms and Crown Wars | Long memory, wounded landscapes, disputed legacy | Medium unless reaffirmed locally |
| Netheril and its fall | Lost high magic, fallen enclaves, magical hubris | High broad truth; verify fine detail |
| Dales Compact and Dale Reckoning | Dalelands identity and dating tradition | High for Dalelands use |
| Time of Troubles | Recent historical proof that divine order can rupture | High broad truth |
| Spellplague | Magical and geographic trauma | High broad truth; verify local scars |
| Second Sundering | Foundation of the present fifth-edition era | High broad truth |
| 2025 sourcebook present | Current reusable package anchor | High anchor, medium exact year |

## Everyday Life Defaults

These are questions and ranges, not universal answers.

- Calendar: Harptos-style months, tendays, and festival days may structure dated play; explain ordinary equivalents when clarity matters.
- Currency: metal coinage is broadly legible, while local minting, trust, barter, credit, and scarcity can change practical value.
- Food and work: derive from climate, trade, faith, class, and local magic rather than generic tavern menus.
- Literacy: variable by region, institution, profession, and class.
- Healing: mundane care, community practice, alchemy, temples, and spellcasting coexist with unequal access.
- Religion: people may honor different deities for travel, harvest, craft, sea, justice, birth, or death.
- Law: define who investigates, judges, punishes, and can be bought or appealed to.
- News: define its carrier, delay, distortion, censorship, and price.
- Magic access: assign a regional band and name who controls advanced services.
- Adventurers: define whether they are admired, licensed, feared, exploited, or treated as armed outsiders.

## Observability

A pressure should become visible through at least two channels before it causes irreversible harm:

- Changed prices, shortages, tolls, or work patterns
- Missing travelers, altered patrols, or closed roads
- Public sermons, proclamations, or rumors
- Ecological or magical symptoms
- Faction recruitment, intimidation, or unusual generosity
- Contradictory testimony from affected communities

## Power Limits

- A powerful NPC must have obligations, uncertainty, opposition, or a reason the party's agency matters.
- Teleportation, resurrection, divination, and magical communication are not assumed to erase distance, death, mystery, or politics.
- A faction's reach is not the same as total control.
- A deity's existence is not proof that a priest's interpretation is correct.
- A historical claim is not automatically a legitimate present claim.
- High-level threats should emerge from accumulated consequences rather than opening spectacle by default.

## Adaptation Seams

The following can change without breaking the setting core:

- Exact current year
- Selected outcomes of published adventures
- Magic-service availability
- Visibility of gods and omens
- Survival pressure
- Political darkness
- Use of famous characters
- Degree of planar travel
- Rules implementation

Record every selected value in campaign-owned files rather than modifying this reference package during play.
