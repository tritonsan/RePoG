# Cyberpunk Universe Campaign Frames

## Use Contract

Every frame below is RePoG-original campaign design, not Cyberpunk canon and not a summary of a published mission, anime episode, or video-game quest. A frame remains selectable reference content until Session 0 accepts it. Player-facing sections may be presented without the GM-only hidden truth.

Famous characters, canonical Fixers, named protagonists, and global corporate leaders are neither patrons nor solutions by default. Every placeholder actor receives a name, identity, appearance, relationship, and local context only during materialization.

## `cyberpunk-frame-last-light` — Last Light On The Block

- Status: package-original proposal
- Compatible continuity: `cyberpunk-2045-red`
- Opening theater: `cyberpunk-region-rebuilding-district`
- Optional connection layer: `cyberpunk-region-citinet-layer`
- Crew mandate: original mixed crew retained by a district utility cooperative, hired separately by affected residents, or personally tied to the neighborhood
- Opening scale: one housing cluster, one clinic, one utility junction, one workshop, one depot, and one disputed shipment

### Player-Facing Promise

A rebuilding district has forty-eight hours before a failing power-and-water control unit shuts down the clinic, pumps, and half the occupied housing. The replacement vanished between a Nomad handoff and a local depot. A corporate subcontractor claims any recovered unit belongs to its megabuilding project, while the security provider threatens to seize “stolen property.” Find a working replacement, establish who has a right to it, and decide whether keeping the district alive requires theft, repair, exposure, bargaining, or a new arrangement.

### Visible Situation

- The existing controller is failing in observable stages; repair can buy hours, not solve the underlying damage.
- A shipment manifest lists two visually identical control units under one damaged cargo record.
- The utility cooperative has payment receipts but no recognized corporate purchase order.
- The subcontractor has an authenticated ownership claim tied to a different construction deadline.
- The local security provider will enforce the subcontractor's claim after its next shift change.
- A Nomad courier reached the city but refuses to surrender route telemetry without protection for a missing driver.
- A Fixer can source an inferior substitute, but its installation would permanently reduce service to part of the district.

### Actor Configuration

- Utility cooperative: knows the physical system and whom failure will harm
- Resident or clinic committee: paid through pooled funds and expects accountability
- Nomad courier: delivered part of the load and is searching for a driver
- Local Fixer: brokered the original purchase and protects supplier confidence
- Corporate subcontractor: needs a controller to avoid default on a separate project
- Security supervisor: has a narrow seizure contract and doubts the paperwork
- Depot technician: handled the damaged manifest and concealed one mistake

### Causal Chain

1. A storm and bridge failure forced the convoy to divide its cargo between two vehicles.
2. The original digital manifest was already corrupted by a supplier's duplicate serial entry.
3. The missing driver diverted to rescue stranded travelers, then lost communications after vehicle damage.
4. At the depot, a technician saw two claims against one serial and relabeled the unit already received to keep it from automatic corporate seizure.
5. The cooperative's pooled payment is genuine, but the Fixer bought surplus stock before the subcontractor's procurement office completed its own claim.
6. The subcontractor's project and the neighborhood therefore both possess credible but incomplete rights to different units that records now treat as one.
7. The security provider knows only the authenticated corporate serial and will worsen the emergency if it seizes the installed substitute.
8. No single mastermind stole the shipment; damaged infrastructure, duplicate records, rescue, triage, and defensive relabeling produced the crisis.

### Neutral Action Space

The crew may repair the current controller, locate the driver, recover the second unit, reconstruct custody, negotiate shared or staged use, expose procurement failures, protect the depot technician, buy the inferior substitute, redirect corporate inventory, arrange temporary generation, challenge the security contract, or help the district accept reduced service. No faction owns the correct answer. A legal claim does not erase immediate need, and immediate need does not prove every taking harmless.

### Inaction Trajectory

- First deadline: rolling failures shut down lifts, refrigeration, and part of the water system.
- Second deadline: clinic reserves and housing safety become critical.
- Third deadline: security seizes the controller at the depot or utility junction.
- End state: the district fragments into unequal service islands, the subcontractor still misses its project deadline, and the missing driver remains stranded.

### PC Integration Prompts

- What capability gives you standing here: repair, medicine, transport, negotiation, investigation, media, security access, or force?
- Which person or service in the district matters to you, if you want that tie in play?
- What kind of property claim will you not enforce merely because a contract is valid?
- What debt, favor, or reputation connects one crew member to the Fixer or courier?
- What resource do you contribute without surrendering control of your character's history?

### GM-Only Hidden Truth

Hidden-truth id: `cyberpunk-frame-last-light-secret-001`

There is no theft conspiracy and neither claimant's paperwork is wholly false. Two real units exist. A duplicated supplier serial, convoy split, rescue diversion, depot relabeling, and asynchronous corporate procurement collapsed them into one record. The missing driver is alive with the second damaged unit and stranded travelers. The depot technician concealed a relabeling to stop automatic seizure, not to sell the component. Reconstructing the route can preserve both projects if the crew also solves transport and repair; choosing only which claimant “owns” the visible unit leaves the larger system broken.

### Reveal Requirements

Establish at least three independent chains:

1. supplier invoice → duplicate serial → procurement timestamps
2. convoy telemetry → bridge failure → split cargo → rescue route
3. depot intake → relabeling → security seizure rule → current physical unit

A confession, one hacked file, or violence against the security team is insufficient.

### Safe Foreshadowing

- Weight records show the convoy carried more than one controller.
- The two claim timestamps cannot describe the same physical handoff.
- The courier asks about people before cargo.
- The depot label adhesive is newer than the casing scratches.
- The security supervisor repeatedly requests a serial rather than describing the object.

### Forbidden Early Wording

Do not call the courier a thief, the technician a corporate agent, the cooperative's receipt fake, the subcontractor's claim fraudulent, or the Fixer the mastermind before evidence supports it.

### Boundaries And Review

Discuss infrastructure failure, medical risk, housing danger, armed seizure, poverty, employer pressure, possible vehicle injury, and whether residents may be displaced. No PC's personal tie is endangered unless that player opts in.

### Expansion

Expand to the port/industrial corridor or Badlands only through the active cargo route. Expand to city politics only if the crew publishes the procurement failure or challenges the district's contract structure. Do not introduce a famous Fixer or corporate legend to arbitrate.

## `cyberpunk-frame-market-ghost` — Ghost Stock

- Status: package-original proposal
- Compatible continuity: `cyberpunk-2045-red`
- Opening theater: `cyberpunk-region-night-market`
- Optional connection layer: `cyberpunk-region-citinet-layer`
- Crew mandate: market operators, Tech/Medtech inspectors, Fixer associates, protectors, investigators, or affected buyers
- Opening scale: one market night, one clinic, one storage site, one supplier route, and one damaged buyer network

### Player-Facing Promise

A respected Night Market closes early after three people suffer dangerous failures from supposedly verified neural-interface modules. The serials belong to a legitimate corporate lot destroyed on paper months ago. Vendors accuse a rival Fixer, buyers demand names, and the host neighborhood threatens to bar the market permanently. Trace the stock before another implant fails—and decide whether saving the market means exposing its suppliers, compensating buyers, protecting a whistleblower, or ending the trade.

### Visible Situation

- The failed modules share a manufacturing lot but entered through different vendors.
- Two failures occurred after professional installation; user error is not a sufficient explanation.
- A clinic has preserved diagnostic evidence but will not reveal patient identities without consent.
- A corporate recovery team offers payment for all units and demands silence.
- The market's invitation list was copied, but the copy predates the first sale.
- A vendor disappeared after warning that “ghost stock” was moving through several districts.
- Buyers need compatible replacements and cannot simply remove every implant safely.

### Actor Configuration

- Market host or Fixer: protects reputation and continued neighborhood access
- Affected buyers: possess different needs, risks, and desired remedies
- Medtech or clinic: holds consent-bound diagnostic evidence
- Vendor whistleblower: traced the lot and went into hiding
- Supplier broker: combined surplus and rejected stock without full testing
- Corporate recovery office: wants proprietary failure evidence contained
- Host neighborhood: bears violence, traffic, and medical consequences from the market

### Causal Chain

1. A manufacturer rejected a module lot after intermittent defects but outsourced destruction.
2. A disposal contractor instead sold sealed pallets as salvage through two intermediaries.
3. A supplier broker mixed those pallets with legitimate surplus and relied on intact packaging as verification.
4. The whistleblower noticed serial clustering, copied the invitation list to warn likely buyers, and disappeared after a threat from the disposal contractor.
5. One affected buyer modified their module, but that alteration explains only one symptom, not the shared defect.
6. The corporate recovery office wants the modules back to prevent liability and industrial leakage; it did not seed the failures.
7. Market practices enabled the spread, but no rival Fixer engineered the crisis.

### Neutral Action Space

The crew may recover stock, protect the whistleblower, obtain patient-approved diagnostics, compel or negotiate compensation, expose the disposal chain, preserve market confidentiality, reform verification, shut the market, publish the defect, arrange replacements, or sell evidence. Protecting a source may conflict with rapid recall; preserving the market may conflict with public accountability.

### Inaction Trajectory

A fourth failure causes public violence, the host neighborhood expels the market, corporate recovery destroys traceable evidence, and vendors blame one another while defective stock moves elsewhere.

### PC Integration Prompts

- What makes you trusted—or distrusted—at this market?
- What piece of gear or care do you rely on that required informal access?
- Who may know details about your body or cyberware?
- What verification failure would make you stop defending the market?

### GM-Only Hidden Truth

Hidden-truth id: `cyberpunk-frame-market-ghost-secret-001`

The defective stock came from a disposal contractor, not a rival market or intentional corporate field test. The supplier broker mixed lots negligently but did not know the exact defect. The whistleblower copied access data to warn buyers, not to sell them out. Corporate recovery's cover-up and the market's weak verification are separate forms of self-protection that now reinforce each other.

### Reveal Requirements

Require patient-consented diagnostics, serial/provenance comparison, and physical or financial evidence from the disposal chain. One corporate file or confession cannot establish every vendor's knowledge.

### Safe Foreshadowing

- Packaging dates differ while serials cluster.
- The invitation copy contains warning annotations, not prices.
- Disposal invoices describe weight rather than item count.
- Recovery agents ask for diagnostic media before they ask about patients.

### Forbidden Early Wording

Do not call the modules a deliberate weapon, the whistleblower a data thief, every vendor complicit, or the corporation the sole cause before discovery.

### Boundaries And Review

Discuss medical harm, surgery, bodily privacy, defective assistive technology, corporate ownership, black-market healthcare, violence, and consent to reveal patient data.

### Expansion

Follow the actual disposal and supplier route only. Do not turn the defect into an unsupported AI infection, cyberpsychosis trigger, or Blackwall event.

## `cyberpunk-frame-open-channel` — Keep The Channel Open

- Status: package-original proposal
- Compatible continuity: `cyberpunk-2045-red`; a later-era adaptation requires new technology assumptions
- Opening theater: `cyberpunk-region-entertainment-media`
- Crew mandate: Media investigation team, Rockerboy movement, venue workers, protectors, community organizers, or mixed crew safeguarding evidence and people
- Opening scale: one district cleanup site, one venue, one independent channel, one corporate media office, and one publication deadline

### Player-Facing Promise

An independent Media obtains footage suggesting that a rebuilding contractor knowingly cut utilities during an occupied-block clearance, causing deaths omitted from the official report. The recording is incomplete, the source has vanished, and a popular performer plans to broadcast it during a live show. The contractor calls it a fake; a gang claims responsibility for unrelated violence in the same footage; and workers fear exposure will cost them every job. Verify what happened, protect the people who choose to speak, and decide what can be published before the channel is silenced.

### Visible Situation

- The footage clearly shows a utility shutdown and later emergency response, but not who gave the order.
- Official casualty numbers conflict with clinic intake and worker messages.
- The missing source accessed maintenance systems but not executive communications.
- The performer can draw an audience but cannot verify evidence.
- A corporate outlet offers to buy exclusive rights and delay release for “authentication.”
- Security surveils the venue under a safety contract.
- A gang's public claim mentions an explosion that occurred after the utility failure.

### Actor Configuration

- Independent Media: holds the footage and protects the source
- Performer/Rockerboy network: offers reach and mobilization
- Cleanup workers: know the sequence but face retaliation
- Contractor office: wants the project and public narrative preserved
- Corporate media editor: wants exclusivity, credibility, and institutional control
- Security provider: has access logs and a venue contract
- Gang spokesperson: claims status from a separate act of violence

### Causal Chain

1. A project manager ordered an unscheduled utility cutoff to accelerate clearance after residents refused relocation.
2. A subcontractor failed to verify occupancy and disabled emergency power.
3. Several deaths resulted from the outage and delayed response, while a later gang attack caused additional casualties.
4. The missing source copied maintenance footage and worker messages, then entered hiding after security questioned colleagues.
5. Senior corporate leadership did not issue the cutoff but began suppressing evidence once liability became clear.
6. The gang inflates its role; the official report collapses all casualties into gang violence.
7. The corporate outlet can authenticate the material but intends to remove worker testimony and protect advertisers.

### Neutral Action Space

The crew may verify timestamps, locate the source, obtain worker consent, negotiate protection, stage or cancel the broadcast, release partial evidence, confront the contractor, use the corporate outlet, preserve anonymity, force a public hearing, compensate families, or trade the story for material reforms. Publication is a decision with effects, not an automatic victory.

### Inaction Trajectory

The live show proceeds with unverified claims, security raids the channel, the gang becomes the accepted explanation, workers lose access to records, and the contractor resumes clearance under stronger protection.

### PC Integration Prompts

- Which audience, workplace, or community trusts you?
- What source-protection rule will you not break?
- What would make you delay a true story?
- What performance, message, or public risk do you want your character to own?

### GM-Only Hidden Truth

Hidden-truth id: `cyberpunk-frame-open-channel-secret-001`

The contractor's project manager caused the initial lethal outage; the gang caused a later explosion; and senior leadership's cover-up began after, not before, the deaths. The missing source is alive and hiding with incomplete evidence. The corporate outlet can prove the sequence but will publish a sanitized account. No single clip contains the whole truth.

### Reveal Requirements

Require utility logs, clinic or responder chronology, worker testimony, and separation of outage casualties from later violence. Respect source and patient consent; hacking one archive does not supply context held by workers.

### Safe Foreshadowing

- Emergency calls begin before the claimed gang attack.
- Contractor messages use clearance metrics rather than security language.
- The source copied maintenance data but never opened executive files.
- The corporate editor asks which names can be removed before discussing authenticity.

### Forbidden Early Wording

Do not call the source dead, the footage fake or complete, the gang innocent of all harm, or corporate leadership the original author of the cutoff before evidence supports it.

### Boundaries And Review

Discuss workplace deaths, displacement, censorship, retaliation, surveillance, public exposure, performance risk, and whether casualty detail remains off-screen.

### Expansion

Expand to citywide politics only through the publication's actual audience and response. A famous Rockerboy or Media does not endorse or rescue the story.

## `cyberpunk-frame-broken-convoy` — The Convoy That Arrived Twice

- Status: package-original proposal
- Compatible continuity: `cyberpunk-2045-red`
- Opening theaters: `cyberpunk-region-badlands-route` and one endpoint in `cyberpunk-region-port-industrial`
- Crew mandate: Nomad convoy members, Badlands couriers, mechanics, Medtechs, escorts, Fixers, or city specialists traveling under a negotiated compact
- Opening scale: two convoy fragments, one damaged bridge route, one city depot, one settlement, and one medical cargo

### Player-Facing Promise

A medical convoy is declared delivered in Night City while the actual refrigerated cargo remains stranded beyond a collapsed route with injured travelers. A second vehicle carrying cloned credentials entered the depot first and sold an inferior shipment. The city client refuses to pay twice; the Nomad unit's leadership orders the crew to protect the family's reputation; and a settlement along the route needs part of the medicine now. Recover the cargo, identify the false delivery, and decide who receives supplies that cannot satisfy everyone.

### Visible Situation

- The city depot authenticated the first vehicle's credentials.
- Temperature telemetry from the real cargo stops near the route collapse.
- The settlement treated survivors and asks for medicine in exchange for continued help.
- A family elder orders silence until the credential breach is understood.
- The false shipment contains usable but diluted medicine.
- The city clinic will exhaust reserves before a replacement convoy can arrive.
- A corporate client threatens to transfer future routes to a rival carrier.

### Actor Configuration

- Stranded convoy lead: protects people, cargo, and family trust
- City clinic: needs medicine and verifiable custody
- Route settlement: provides rescue and claims an immediate share
- Nomad decision-maker: guards reputation and future contracts
- Credential broker: copied access for what they believed was harmless cargo fraud
- Rival carrier or criminal crew: executed the false delivery
- Corporate procurement officer: values continuity and deniability over nuance

### Causal Chain

1. A broker copied convoy credentials to facilitate low-risk customs avoidance for a separate shipment.
2. A criminal crew bought the credentials and used them to sell diluted medicine before the real convoy arrived.
3. The real route collapsed after weather and neglected infrastructure, injuring travelers and damaging refrigeration.
4. The settlement diverted power and labor to preserve cargo and lives.
5. A family elder learned of the earlier credential copy and concealed it to avoid losing contracts.
6. The clinic accepted the false shipment because credentials and packaging passed, then discovered dilution during use.
7. Recovery now requires both physical rescue and an account of institutional failures; no single rival planned the collapse.

### Neutral Action Space

The crew may rescue travelers, repair refrigeration, allocate medicine, expose or quietly correct the credential breach, recover payment, confront the false-delivery crew, compensate the settlement, preserve or challenge family leadership, negotiate staged supply, or publish the clinic's intake evidence.

### Inaction Trajectory

The cargo spoils, the settlement withdraws assistance, the clinic rationing harms patients, the corporate client blacklists the carrier, and the credential broker sells another copy to escape debt.

### PC Integration Prompts

- What role do you hold in or beside the transport compact?
- Which obligation outranks the cargo for your character?
- What does family or crew reputation mean to you, if anything?
- What route, vehicle, patient, or settlement detail may become personally important with your consent?

### GM-Only Hidden Truth

Hidden-truth id: `cyberpunk-frame-broken-convoy-secret-001`

The rival crew exploited credentials copied by a broker, but neither caused the route collapse. A respected family elder concealed the original credential leak. The settlement preserved most of the real cargo and has a legitimate claim for labor and immediate need. The city clinic acted reasonably on authenticated data. Resolving only the theft leaves the trust, route, and triage conflicts intact.

### Reveal Requirements

Require credential version history, cargo chemistry or medical verification, route evidence, and testimony from settlement rescuers. Family rank alone does not establish truth.

### Safe Foreshadowing

- The false vehicle's maintenance profile does not match the credentials.
- The real cargo's temperature curve shows emergency power cycling.
- The settlement requests specific doses rather than a percentage of value.
- The elder knows when credentials were copied but not who bought them.

### Forbidden Early Wording

Do not call the settlement extortionists, the clinic complicit, the whole rival carrier responsible, or the family elder a traitor before discovery.

### Boundaries And Review

Discuss medical scarcity, triage, vehicle injury, family hierarchy, criminal fraud, patient harm, and whether medicine allocation is played directly or abstracted.

### Expansion

Follow one active route, client, or credential buyer. Do not generalize one unit's conduct to every Nomad family or nation.

## `cyberpunk-frame-clean-hands` — Clean Hands, Dirty Contract

- Status: package-original proposal
- Compatible continuity: `cyberpunk-2045-red`
- Opening theater: `cyberpunk-region-civic-executive`
- Crew mandate: emergency responders, Trauma-Team-adjacent or independent medics, security personnel, Lawman-like investigators, internal-affairs contractors, or mixed crew under a limited mandate
- Opening scale: one executive boundary, one public block, one emergency dispatch system, one clinic, and one security contract

### Player-Facing Promise

A security response at an executive-zone boundary leaves residents injured while the protected client receives immediate medical evacuation. Dispatch records show that community calls were downgraded before the incident, but every operator denies changing them. The provider hires your crew to find a rogue employee before an oversight hearing; residents demand the raw logs; and field responders warn that exposing the system could end the only service that still answers some calls. Determine who changed priority, why force escalated, and whether accountability can preserve care without preserving the contract.

### Visible Situation

- Body-camera, dispatch, clinic, and witness timelines disagree by several minutes.
- Responders followed a priority code that appears valid in their system.
- A supervisor's credentials were used while that supervisor was physically elsewhere.
- The protected client funded a recent software upgrade.
- Community medics treated people whom the official report omits.
- One field team member removed a recording before internal collection.
- The oversight hearing begins after the next operational shift.

### Actor Configuration

- Security provider management: wants a bounded rogue-employee explanation
- Field responders: hold different views about the contract and use of force
- Community medics and injured residents: possess treatment and witness evidence
- Executive client office: financed priority automation and demands service guarantees
- Dispatch technician: understands the upgrade and fears employer retaliation
- District oversight representative: needs evidence but may trade accountability for continuity
- Data custodian: holds backups whose release could expose unrelated patients and callers

### Causal Chain

1. The executive client paid for an optimization layer that systematically raised threats near protected assets and lowered ambiguous calls outside the boundary.
2. Provider management approved performance targets without documenting their discriminatory operational effect.
3. A dispatcher used a supervisor credential to override one community call upward, not downward.
4. The system later reversed that override during a client alert, producing the disputed priority code.
5. Field responders escalated force based on incomplete threat tags; one responder then removed a recording to protect an injured colleague and a resident visible in it.
6. Community medics' records prove timing and harm but contain sensitive patient information.
7. There is no single rogue operator; contract design, automation, management incentives, field decisions, and evidence protection all contributed.

### Neutral Action Space

The crew may reconstruct the system, protect patients, expose the contract, negotiate service reform, identify individual misconduct, preserve emergency coverage, support the hearing, release redacted evidence, resign the mandate, side with responders or residents, or force the client to fund independent care.

### Inaction Trajectory

Management blames the dispatcher, the client renews the optimization layer, responders lose community cooperation, the hearing closes on incomplete evidence, and future calls receive even less reliable response.

### PC Integration Prompts

- What authority or expertise lets you inspect this incident?
- What order will your character refuse despite a contract?
- Which evidence category—medical, operational, personal, or corporate—must remain consent-bound?
- What does accountability mean if shutting the provider harms people who still depend on it?

### GM-Only Hidden Truth

Hidden-truth id: `cyberpunk-frame-clean-hands-secret-001`

The dispatcher tried to help; the client-funded priority layer reversed the intervention when the protected alert arrived. Management knowingly accepted distorted performance incentives but did not order this specific harm. A responder removed one recording for mixed reasons: to shield a colleague from discipline and to protect an injured resident's identity. The logs can establish the system failure only when joined to community medical evidence.

### Reveal Requirements

Require software change records, dispatch version comparison, field timeline, and patient-consented medical chronology. A confession or raw data dump without interpretation is insufficient.

### Safe Foreshadowing

- Downgrades cluster geographically rather than by dispatcher.
- The supervisor credential first raises priority before the system lowers it.
- Client alerts use a different clock source.
- The missing recording's metadata shows two intended recipients.

### Forbidden Early Wording

Do not call the dispatcher corrupt, the field team uniformly malicious, the automation sentient, or the residents' account complete before evidence is joined.

### Boundaries And Review

Discuss policing, private security, medical triage, surveillance, algorithmic discrimination, injury, institutional retaliation, patient privacy, and employment loss.

### Expansion

Expand only through the actual client, provider, oversight office, or affected district. Do not invoke MAX-TAC, Trauma Team, NCPD, or Militech unless the group selects and researches that named institution.

## `cyberpunk-frame-shattered-loyalty` — A Country In The Dead Drop

- Status: package-original high-complexity proposal
- Compatible continuity: `cyberpunk-2077-phantom-liberty` or a separately researched 2077 branch
- Opening theaters: `cyberpunk-region-dogtown` and `cyberpunk-region-quickhack-layer`
- Crew mandate: original espionage, political, quickhack, extraction, Media, or defectors' support team
- Opening scale: one threatened source, one border route, one dead drop, two intelligence claims, and one extraction deadline

### Player-Facing Promise

A technical analyst inside Dogtown offers evidence that a security system sold as defensive is quietly mapping civilian implants for an intelligence buyer. The analyst demands extraction beyond the enclave and names both an NUS handler and a local power broker as likely assassins. Each offers your crew a different immunity deal, and a quickhack already circulating under the analyst's signature has injured people. Determine what the analyst built, who bought it, and whether extraction protects a whistleblower, a compromised operative, or the author of the attack.

### Visible Situation

- The analyst possesses genuine design knowledge and fears immediate capture.
- The circulating quickhack uses code from the defensive system but includes later modifications.
- The NUS-linked handler claims the system prevents larger attacks.
- A local broker offers a safe route in exchange for the data archive.
- Civilian victims show a geographic pattern the analyst cannot explain.
- One dead drop contains conflicting orders signed with valid credentials.
- Extraction requires crossing a politically controlled boundary before a source-specific deadline.

### Actor Configuration

- Analyst/defector: built part of the system and withholds one decision
- NUS-linked handler: wants the analyst, archive, and strategic deniability
- Dogtown-local broker or authority: wants leverage and control over the route
- Victim network or clinic: holds bodily and geographic evidence
- Quickhack distributor: altered and sold weaponized code
- Crew's extraction contact: can move people but not every copy of the data
- Independent Media or investigator: can expose the program at high source risk

### Causal Chain

1. The analyst designed a defensive telemetry tool that maps compatible implant signatures with nominal anonymization.
2. An intelligence buyer secretly expanded retention and linked signatures to movement patterns.
3. The analyst discovered the expansion and copied the archive, but previously shared a diagnostic module with a trusted colleague.
4. That colleague sold the module; a distributor weaponized it into the circulating quickhack.
5. The victims cluster near test infrastructure, not because the analyst selected them.
6. The NUS-linked handler wants to preserve the program while extracting the analyst; the local broker wants the archive as bargaining leverage.
7. Conflicting dead-drop orders come from two authorized offices competing inside the same intelligence structure.
8. The analyst is a whistleblower with real responsibility for enabling the later harm, not a secret mastermind or innocent cipher.

### Neutral Action Space

The crew may extract the analyst, prioritize victims, destroy or split the archive, expose the system, negotiate immunity, identify the distributor, hand evidence to either power, create a controlled disclosure, refuse the job, or help the analyst testify. No agency guarantees safety, and no single data transfer ends every copy.

### Inaction Trajectory

The analyst is captured, the distributor releases a wider version, both offices blame Dogtown actors, the local broker closes the route, and victims become evidence in a political escalation they do not control.

### PC Integration Prompts

- What gives you access to this operation without making you a published protagonist's substitute?
- Which institution, nation, employer, or community may claim your loyalty but not own it?
- What bodily or network intrusion is off-limits for your character?
- Who decides whether the crew accepts immunity, exposure, or extraction risk?

### GM-Only Hidden Truth

Hidden-truth id: `cyberpunk-frame-shattered-loyalty-secret-001`

The analyst built the mapping foundation, an intelligence office removed its privacy limits, and an independent distributor weaponized leaked diagnostic code. Two valid offices issued contradictory orders. The victims were exposed by test geography, not targeted identity. The analyst deserves protection from assassination and accountability for negligent sharing; neither the NUS handler nor local broker possesses the entire causal chain.

### Reveal Requirements

Require source-supported 2077 technical rules, versioned code comparison, victim-consented clinic data, dead-drop credential analysis, and evidence of the separate retention expansion. Quickhacking one actor cannot reveal knowledge they never held.

### Safe Foreshadowing

- Harmful code contains later libraries absent from the analyst's build.
- Victim geography follows infrastructure coverage.
- Both dead-drop signatures validate but use different office conventions.
- The handler asks for the person and archive; the broker asks only for leverage.

### Forbidden Early Wording

Do not call the analyst innocent, traitorous, or the attacker; do not call either office “the NUSA”; do not label the quickhack a Blackwall or rogue-AI event without sourced evidence.

### Boundaries And Review

Discuss espionage, state violence, assassination, coercive immunity, bodily hacking, surveillance, political betrayal, patient privacy, and *Phantom Liberty* spoilers. Define quickhack effects and player-body boundaries before selection.

### Expansion

This frame requires a targeted 2077 source pass. It may not import V, Johnny, Songbird, Reed, the NUS President, or another published character as patron or solution. Any intersection with a published quest requires an explicit divergence record.

## Frame Materialization Rule

Materialize exactly one frame. Copy its player-facing promise, selected actors, causal chain, clocks, and protected hidden truth into appropriate campaign owners. Do not copy alternate frames or their secrets. Rename every placeholder through Session 0; a package role is not an accepted person.
