# Cyberpunk Universe World Operating Model

## Use Contract

These are durable causal models for a future Cyberpunk campaign, not active state and not a substitute for an owned rulebook or era source. Materialize only the truths that affect the selected opening. Every district, route, service, organization, Net, implant, crew, and clock must be instantiated locally.

## Core Causal Chain

1. Historical violence and institutional choices shape present infrastructure, borders, records, and distrust.
2. People need food, water, power, shelter, healthcare, transport, communication, identity documentation, work, and safety.
3. Those needs move through fragile systems owned, maintained, contested, or improvised by specific actors.
4. Scarcity creates brokerage, barter, Night Markets, contracts, smuggling, salvage, repair, and unequal access.
5. Corporations, governments, gangs, security providers, Nomads, Fixers, criminal networks, and communities gain power by controlling different links rather than everything.
6. Cyberware and network technology expand possibility while creating maintenance, access, ownership, privacy, and bodily-autonomy questions.
7. Information is local, copied, interpreted, sold, hidden, and damaged; no institution sees the entire city.
8. Crews form because mobility, technical skill, care, violence, negotiation, media reach, and local trust rarely belong to one person.
9. Every job changes relationships and dependencies even when no official territory changes hands.
10. Violence can remove a person but cannot automatically replace their labor, knowledge, legitimacy, route, or community role.
11. Style, art, performance, language, and chosen affiliation are ways people assert identity under systems that classify and commodify them.
12. A local choice about one shipment, clinic, signal, contract, body, or witness can propagate into district and city politics.
13. The selected era determines what technology and institutions can plausibly do.
14. The crew's decisions—not a famous protagonist—determine the campaign's local future.

## Durable Truth Cards

### `cyberpunk-truth-001` — History Has Material Weight

- Status: template-fixed; exact history is era-specific
- Truth: wars, corporate projects, disasters, migrations, and rebuilding leave physical and institutional consequences.
- Table consequence: a ruin, missing record, broken route, contaminated site, or disputed title must have a dated cause and current holder.
- Must not imply: every present conflict is secretly controlled by one canonical event or famous person.
- Open question: which past decision still constrains this district today?
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-night-city-history`, `claim-cyberpunk-war-datakrash`.

### `cyberpunk-truth-002` — Infrastructure Is Power

- Status: template-fixed
- Truth: power, water, CitiNet, roads, ports, clinics, housing, waste, transport, and security operate through people and physical systems.
- Table consequence: controlling a service creates leverage only while workers, parts, routes, and users sustain it.
- Must not imply: a corporation owns every dependency or a single hack controls an entire city.
- Open question: who maintains the service, who receives it, and where can it fail?
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-2045-governance`, `claim-cyberpunk-district-variation`, `claim-cyberpunk-infrastructure-rebuild`.

### `cyberpunk-truth-003` — Scarcity Has A Route

- Status: template-fixed in 2045; degree is campaign-instance
- Truth: scarce goods arrive through procurement, transport, brokerage, repair, substitution, salvage, or theft.
- Table consequence: a desired component creates actors, delays, prices, obligations, and alternatives rather than appearing from an abstract shop list.
- Must not imply: every item is unavailable, money is useless, or Fixers can conjure inventory.
- Open question: what route produced this item and who bears the risk?
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-scarcity-economy`, `claim-cyberpunk-night-markets-fixers`, `claim-cyberpunk-nomad-logistics`.

### `cyberpunk-truth-004` — Ordinary People Are Causal Actors

- Status: template-fixed
- Truth: Night City is sustained and changed by people whose lives do not center on mercenary work.
- Table consequence: workers, residents, patients, drivers, medics, performers, vendors, organizers, and caretakers possess goals, knowledge, refusal, and follow-through.
- Must not imply: bystanders are scenery, communities need a crew to speak for them, or combat skill grants legitimacy.
- Open question: what will local people do if the crew never arrives?
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-everyday-life`, `claim-cyberpunk-housing-work`.

### `cyberpunk-truth-005` — Bodies Are Lived, Not Inventories

- Status: template-fixed ethical boundary; capabilities are player- and era-specific
- Truth: cyberware, organic tissue, assistive devices, medication, fashion, and full-body conversion concern a person's body and agency.
- Table consequence: installation, maintenance, repair, ownership claims, medical access, and unwanted modification have social and personal stakes.
- Must not imply: more chrome means less personhood, an unaugmented body is purer, or visible augmentation predicts violence.
- Open question: who chose, paid for, maintains, benefits from, and claims authority over the modification?
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-cyberware-body`, `claim-cyberpunk-humanity-therapy`.

### `cyberpunk-truth-006` — Information Has Custody

- Status: template-fixed
- Truth: data, testimony, recordings, manifests, identity records, and rumors exist in copies held and interpreted by particular people or systems.
- Table consequence: finding, deleting, altering, or publishing one copy changes only part of the evidence chain.
- Must not imply: one Netrunner knows everything, a successful roll erases every backup, or damaged records mean no one remembers.
- Open question: what was observed, where is it stored, who can interpret it, and what other copy exists?
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-net-2045`, `claim-cyberpunk-media-culture`, `claim-cyberpunk-identities-citizenship`.

### `cyberpunk-truth-007` — Technology Belongs To An Era

- Status: template-fixed
- Truth: Old NET collapse, 2045 CitiNet and local Architectures, and 2070 quickhacking are different conditions.
- Table consequence: every technical plan first names the era, network type, access path, and source-supported capability.
- Must not imply: 2070 wireless quickhacks work in 2045 or every device is remotely accessible.
- Open question: what exact system is present here?
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-war-datakrash`, `claim-cyberpunk-net-2045`, `claim-cyberpunk-net-2070`.

### `cyberpunk-truth-008` — Institutions Are Coalitions

- Status: template-fixed
- Truth: corporations, states, councils, gangs, security providers, Nomad groups, and media organizations contain offices, people, contracts, rivals, and incomplete knowledge.
- Table consequence: an institutional action requires a local decision-maker, capability, route, budget, and limit.
- Must not imply: “Arasaka knows,” “the NUSA wants,” or “the gang attacks” without a specific actor and purpose.
- Open question: who can authorize the next action and who inside disagrees?
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-corporate-change`, `claim-cyberpunk-gangs-security`, `claim-cyberpunk-dogtown-nusa`.

### `cyberpunk-truth-009` — Security Is Uneven And Interested

- Status: template-fixed broadly; provider and response are campaign-instance
- Truth: law enforcement, private security, gang patrols, community defense, and emergency response protect different clients and assets.
- Table consequence: response depends on district, contract, perceived threat, payment, identity, and institutional interest.
- Must not imply: no law exists, every provider is equally corrupt, or force creates public legitimacy.
- Open question: who is protected, from what, and at whose cost?
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-district-variation`, `claim-cyberpunk-gangs-security`, `claim-cyberpunk-healthcare-security`.

### `cyberpunk-truth-010` — A Crew Is Mutual Dependency

- Status: template-fixed structure; relationships are player-authored
- Truth: a crew works because members bring different access, competence, trust, mobility, reputation, or local ties.
- Table consequence: every PC contributes something difficult to replace and exposes the group to a distinct obligation or risk.
- Must not imply: mandatory friendship, one combat role per person, permanent membership, or unbounded betrayal.
- Open question: what practical need keeps this crew together today, and what exit remains possible?
- Source claim: `claim-cyberpunk-edgerunner-agency`.

### `cyberpunk-truth-011` — Violence Cannot Replace A System

- Status: template-fixed
- Truth: killing or defeating an actor does not automatically repair infrastructure, transfer trust, produce medicine, clear a contract, or teach their knowledge.
- Table consequence: force changes custody and fear while creating bodies, witnesses, retaliation, vacancies, and interrupted labor.
- Must not imply: violence is never useful or every conflict has a clean negotiated answer.
- Open question: what still must function after the shooting stops?
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-everyday-life`, `claim-cyberpunk-infrastructure-rebuild`.

### `cyberpunk-truth-012` — Style Is Social Action

- Status: template-fixed theme; expression is player- and culture-authored
- Truth: fashion, music, body presentation, slang, art, media, and performance communicate belonging, resistance, aspiration, and commerce.
- Table consequence: presentation affects recognition and relationship without becoming a morality stat.
- Must not imply: one authentic Cyberpunk look, compulsory coolness, or cultural traits assigned from ethnicity.
- Open question: who reads this style, and what does the character intend it to say?
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-media-culture`, `claim-cyberpunk-2020-corporate-order`.

### `cyberpunk-truth-013` — Citizenship And Identity Are Material

- Status: template-fixed broadly; exact law is campaign-instance
- Truth: legal identity, records, documentation loss, employment, residence, and district recognition affect access and vulnerability.
- Table consequence: a missing or contested identity changes services, work, movement, policing, and ownership claims.
- Must not imply: every undocumented person is invisible, criminal, or powerless.
- Open question: which institution recognizes this person and which record is disputed?
- Source claim: `claim-cyberpunk-identities-citizenship`.

### `cyberpunk-truth-014` — Famous People Do Not Own The Future

- Status: template-fixed default
- Truth: published protagonists and legends establish parts of the universe but do not determine every local conflict.
- Table consequence: original crews receive consequential choices that no cameo resolves.
- Must not imply: named characters are unimportant or their source-defined histories may be overwritten without divergence.
- Open question: what changes because this crew acted?
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-named-character-continuity`, `claim-cyberpunk-edgerunner-agency`.

## District Operating Model

For every active district record:

- era and canonical adjacency
- population and who is excluded from official counts
- governing or coordinating body
- security provider and response limit
- power, water, waste, communications, and transport condition
- housing patterns and displacement pressure
- livelihoods and shift rhythms
- healthcare and emergency access
- food and goods supply routes
- one community institution with independent goals
- one corporation, contractor, gang, or criminal claim
- one media or data custodian
- one recent event with more than one interpretation
- what continues if the crew does nothing

A district rating is an aid, not a total description of every street.

## Supply-Chain Model

For each critical good or service identify:

1. producer or origin
2. procurement authority
3. broker or Fixer
4. transporter and route
5. storage and security
6. final maintainer or distributor
7. intended users
8. payment, barter, debt, or contract
9. substitute or repair option
10. failure point
11. information each actor lacks
12. who bears delay and who profits

A missing shipment is not automatically theft. It may be damage, rerouting, duplicate manifests, corruption, triage, labor action, identity error, or several independent choices.

## Body, Care, And Cyberware Model

For any implant or bodily intervention important to play, record:

- person's chosen description and pronouns
- function: assistive, medical, occupational, expressive, sensory, communicative, defensive, offensive, or mixed
- consent and installation context
- payer and any claimed ownership
- maintenance, medication, therapy, and repair access
- data produced and who can read it
- physical and social limits
- source-supported capability for the selected era
- portrayal boundaries
- what remains player-private

A mechanical Humanity value never authorizes narration of a PC's feelings, morality, or real-world mental health.

## Information And Net Model

Every digital or media operation identifies:

- era
- physical device or system
- whether it is an Agent, CitiNet service, local NET Architecture, 2070 quickhack target, archive, broadcast system, or other sourced category
- connection and access requirement
- controller and ordinary users
- information actually stored
- copies, backups, and offline records
- automated and human interpretation
- intrusion evidence
- capability and limit of the acting character
- physical consequence

“Hack the city,” “the Net says,” and “delete the file” are incomplete actions.

## Everyday-Life Minimum

Every opening must include:

- one housing form and rent or ownership pressure
- one source of food and clean water
- one clinic, medic, pharmacy, or emergency route
- one livelihood unrelated to violence
- one transport dependency
- one power or communications dependency
- one care or mutual-aid relationship
- one leisure, art, faith, sport, or social space
- one place where locals can refuse the crew
- one consequence that unfolds while the crew is elsewhere

## Conflict Model

Cyberpunk conflict moves through seven linked arenas:

1. **Body** — injury, augmentation, care, autonomy, maintenance
2. **Need** — food, water, power, shelter, medicine, work, transport
3. **Access** — credentials, routes, markets, contracts, reputation, identity
4. **Information** — evidence, narrative, privacy, archives, broadcast, Net control
5. **Relationship** — crew trust, family, community, employer, client, debt
6. **Force** — weapons, security, gangs, coercion, state and corporate violence
7. **Future** — rebuilding, ownership, precedent, migration, who gets to remain

A meaningful consequence usually moves at least two arenas. Recovering a component may restore water while validating a corporate ownership claim; publishing a recording may protect workers while exposing a source; winning a firefight may close the only clinic.

## Default Pressure Cycle

For a bounded 2045 opening, define:

- one service a district cannot safely lose
- one component, shipment, worker, or credential now missing
- one community plan already in motion
- one corporate or security claim that is partly valid
- one broker or transporter with incomplete information
- one data discrepancy or damaged record
- one deadline created by physical failure
- one deadline created by an institution
- one choice that cannot satisfy every obligation

Advance pressure through actor capacity and system failure, not arbitrary betrayal.

## Scale Ladder

| Scale | What PCs directly influence | What remains outside default control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Personal | body, debt, one relationship, one device, immediate survival | district services and institutional policy |
| Crew | one job, shared resource, route, client, and local reputation | citywide markets and corporate strategy |
| District | one service network, several institutions, local security and community settlement | whole-city government and national power |
| City | multiple districts, major media narratives, corporate offices and transport networks | global logistics, national order, orbital politics |
| Regional | Badlands, Free State/NUSA routes, Nomad networks, several cities | complete world conditions and all corporate blocs |
| Global/orbital | international or space-based institutions | unsuitable for default; requires targeted source and new campaign contract |

Default opening: crew scale with provisional influence over one district dependency and no guaranteed territory.

## Clock Contract

Every active clock records:

- actor and desired end state
- next action if unopposed
- capability and route
- contract, legitimacy, or story used to justify it
- physical dependency affected
- visible sign
- evidence created, moved, or destroyed
- people who bear the cost
- information the actor lacks
- internal disagreement
- condition that changes the plan
- era-specific technology involved

A clock never advances merely because a corporation, gang, government, or canonical plot is famous.
