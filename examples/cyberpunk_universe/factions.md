# Cyberpunk Universe Factions And Power Networks

## Use Contract

These are stable power models, not active factions and not an exhaustive list of canonical organizations. A campaign instantiates only three or four opening powers. Each receives an era, local face, place, objective, method, capability, dependency, limit, internal disagreement, clock, and knowledge boundary.

No corporation, government, gang, security provider, Nomad family, Fixer network, media outlet, or community is a hive mind.

## Power Cards

### `cyberpunk-faction-district-authority` — Night City District Authority

- Status: 2045 template; exact office and district require source or original-canvas definition
- Public mask: local representation, service coordination, permits, public safety, and rebuilding
- Desire: keep utilities and commerce functioning, maintain a Council voice, secure funding, and survive competing local interests
- Methods: contracts, permits, procurement, service allocation, records, public messaging, negotiation, and selective enforcement
- Capabilities: local administrative access, convening power, service knowledge, district records, and formal claims
- Dependencies: workers, utilities, contractors, residents, security, corporate financing, and perceived legitimacy
- Limits: decentralized city structure, incomplete records, rival representatives, uneven services, corruption, and limited direct force
- Knowledge habits: official files mixed with personal networks, damaged archives, public hearings, and selective reporting
- Local instantiation: define who actually decides, which services are controlled, and what residents can contest
- Hooks: duplicate permit, utility triage, vacant Council seat, procurement fraud, identity dispute, or a security contract nobody remembers approving
- Caution: government is neither absent nor omnipotent; show daylight and administrative consequences
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-2045-governance`, `claim-cyberpunk-identities-citizenship`, `claim-cyberpunk-district-variation`.

### `cyberpunk-faction-megacorp` — Megacorporate Office

- Status: era-specific institution model
- Public mask: employer, investor, technology provider, landlord, security client, logistics customer, or indispensable service
- Desire: secure profit, assets, data, influence, workforce, supply, strategic position, and a favorable account of its actions
- Methods: contracts, employment, procurement, lobbying, security, research, branding, debt, extraction, and deniable intermediaries
- Capabilities: capital, specialists, equipment, guarded facilities, legal pressure, media access, and multi-region connections
- Dependencies: labor, local infrastructure, transport, suppliers, customers, state relationships, and internal authorization
- Limits: rival offices, damaged logistics, budgets, reputation, law, local resistance, incomplete intelligence, and era-specific weakness
- Knowledge habits: compartmentalized reports, ownership records, metrics, proprietary systems, and information shaped for superiors
- Local instantiation: select one named or original office, not an entire global corporation; record its era-correct status
- Hooks: contested property, employer-owned chrome, defective product, black project, extraction, procurement war, or security overreach
- Caution: corporate resources create asymmetry, not narrative omnipotence
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-2020-corporate-order`, `claim-cyberpunk-corporate-change`, `claim-cyberpunk-cyberware-body`.

### `cyberpunk-faction-neocorp` — NeoCorp And Contractor Network

- Status: 2045 template
- Public mask: flexible company rebuilding what old powers lost or abandoned
- Desire: win contracts, establish a brand, control a niche, outmaneuver older firms, and convert crisis into durable status
- Methods: rapid bids, specialized technology, local partnership, aggressive hiring, subcontracting, debt, and risk-taking
- Capabilities: adaptable teams, newer equipment, fewer inherited procedures, and willingness to enter unstable markets
- Dependencies: financing, Nomad logistics, local permits, reputation, scarce expertise, and larger customers
- Limits: shallow reserves, volatile growth, vulnerable supply, founder politics, and pressure from returning MegaCorps
- Knowledge habits: informal executive channels, sales claims, contractor records, and uneven documentation
- Local instantiation: define one product or service and one contract it cannot fulfill alone
- Hooks: failed pilot, hostile acquisition, stolen prototype, dangerous cost-cutting, community partnership, or disputed salvage rights
- Caution: “new” does not mean benevolent, small, or technologically magical
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-corporate-change`, `claim-cyberpunk-nomad-logistics`.

### `cyberpunk-faction-nomad` — Nomad Transport Unit

- Status: 2045 broad template; family, nation, and cultural detail require selected sources
- Public mask: carrier, driver, mechanic, scout, escort, camp, supplier, smuggler, peacekeeper, or mobile contractor
- Desire: keep people and cargo moving, protect reciprocal ties, maintain vehicles and routes, and preserve the unit's decision-making autonomy
- Methods: convoy organization, route intelligence, repair, escort, negotiation, trade, mutual support, and selective refusal
- Capabilities: mobility, distributed expertise, safe stops, transport capacity, practical logistics, and regional relationships
- Dependencies: fuel, parts, drivers, maintenance, family agreement, clients, route access, and weather or infrastructure
- Limits: finite vehicles, internal obligations, casualties, blocked routes, client distrust, and conflict with authorities or rivals
- Knowledge habits: route reports, family memory, radio, face-to-face trust, manifests, and compartmentalized smuggling where selected
- Local instantiation: one convoy, depot, family unit, or carrier team with a reason to accept or reject the job
- Hooks: stranded cargo, family split, false manifest, missing driver, route closure, security contract, or city client who ignores obligations
- Caution: Nomad is not a synonym for criminal, rootless, culturally uniform, or socially isolated
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-nomad-logistics`, `claim-cyberpunk-corporate-change`.

### `cyberpunk-faction-fixer-market` — Fixer And Night Market Network

- Status: 2045 broad template; exact network is local
- Public mask: broker, connector, negotiator, appraiser, market organizer, problem solver, and reputation custodian
- Desire: maintain reliable access, protect trusted relationships, profit from scarcity, and keep clients dependent without destroying the market
- Methods: introductions, bargaining, verification, controlled invitations, favors, credit, barter, information, and security arrangements
- Capabilities: supplier knowledge, cross-boundary contacts, price discovery, discreet venues, and the ability to match needs with routes
- Dependencies: reputation, vendors, transport, customers, protection, honest appraisal, and secrets that remain bounded
- Limits: bad supply, betrayal, counterfeit goods, violence that scares markets away, and clients who bypass agreements
- Knowledge habits: private ledgers, relationship memory, coded listings, partial provenance, and deliberate need-to-know
- Local instantiation: one broker and one market event or service corridor; define what they cannot source
- Hooks: contaminated stock, copied invitation, disputed commission, urgent medicine, counterfeit implant, or supplier under coercion
- Caution: a Fixer does not provide universal inventory or automatic loyalty
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-night-markets-fixers`, `claim-cyberpunk-scarcity-economy`.

### `cyberpunk-faction-gang` — Gang Or Street Association

- Status: broad 2045 model; named gangs require source review
- Public mask: may include protection, identity, entertainment, work, radical cause, neighborhood presence, smuggling, or predation
- Desire: varies by local group; define survival, belonging, income, status, territory, ideology, protection, revenge, or service explicitly
- Methods: reputation, force, patrol, recruitment, performance, market access, extortion, mutual aid, smuggling, and local knowledge
- Capabilities: rapid mobilization, street presence, community relationships, violence, subculture, and knowledge outsiders lack
- Dependencies: members, money, safe places, neighborhood tolerance, leadership, supply, and a story about why the group matters
- Limits: internal factions, casualties, public backlash, security pressure, rivals, and obligations to people around them
- Knowledge habits: rumor, personal loyalty, visible symbols, selective secrecy, and stories that may matter more than formal records
- Local instantiation: define one concrete function, one harm, one constituency, one internal disagreement, and one limit
- Hooks: protection contract, recruitment dispute, venue control, smuggling route, missing member, radicalization, or community rejection
- Caution: never map a gang automatically to ethnicity, poverty, random violence, or moral uniformity
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-gangs-security`, `claim-cyberpunk-everyday-life`.

### `cyberpunk-faction-security` — Security Provider

- Status: 2045 broad model; provider may be public, private, gang-based, Nomad, community, or specialized
- Public mask: protection, emergency response, threat deterrence, asset defense, policing, or contract enforcement
- Desire: fulfill or exploit the contract, preserve funding and reputation, protect selected clients, and control escalation
- Methods: patrol, access control, surveillance, investigation, armed response, checkpoints, detention, and information sharing
- Capabilities: trained personnel, weapons, communications, legal or contractual authority, vehicles, and response procedures
- Dependencies: client payment, staffing, jurisdiction, intelligence, public tolerance, equipment, and command approval
- Limits: contract scope, travel time, competing authorities, corruption, morale, evidence, politics, and threats beyond available force
- Knowledge habits: incident reports, watch lists, camera feeds, dispatch logs, and unofficial officer networks
- Local instantiation: define protected asset, response area, intensity, arrival conditions, and who is excluded
- Hooks: disputed jurisdiction, contract expiration, staged incident, whistleblower, collateral harm, false alert, or protection demand
- Caution: security is not synonymous with safety, legitimacy, or universal law
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-gangs-security`, `claim-cyberpunk-district-variation`, `claim-cyberpunk-healthcare-security`.

### `cyberpunk-faction-criminal` — Criminal Organization

- Status: broad template; named organizations require source review
- Public mask: legitimate business, family enterprise, transport firm, labor broker, market service, political sponsor, or no mask at all
- Desire: control profitable flows, preserve secrecy, influence authorities, discipline partners, and convert vulnerable systems into leverage
- Methods: smuggling, extortion, corruption, identity fraud, counterfeiting, coercive labor, finance, information, and selective violence
- Capabilities: hidden routes, cash or goods, compromised officials, specialized crews, intimidation, and relationships across legal boundaries
- Dependencies: silence, markets, workers, transport, corrupt access, community tolerance or fear, and internal discipline
- Limits: rival groups, law/security pressure, defectors, public exposure, supply disruption, and leaders with competing interests
- Knowledge habits: compartmentalized cells, coded accounts, personal intermediaries, and records designed to mislead
- Local instantiation: one operation and one affected community; do not activate an entire transnational network without source and scale
- Hooks: coerced work crew, counterfeit identity, stolen cargo, protection dispute, whistleblower, or pilot scheme expanding into a district
- Caution: do not glamorize trafficking, torture, or ethnic stereotype as organizational color
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-gangs-security`, `claim-cyberpunk-identities-citizenship`.

### `cyberpunk-faction-community` — Community And Labor Network

- Status: mandatory causal layer, never one homogeneous faction
- Public mask: none; these are residents, workers, patients, families, organizers, shopkeepers, caregivers, artists, and service users
- Desire: safety, livelihood, dignity, care, reliable services, belonging, justice, privacy, opportunity, and countless goals unrelated to the crew
- Methods: labor, mutual aid, refusal, reporting, protest, repair, voting or representation, commerce, cultural practice, and collective memory
- Capabilities: local trust, daylight action, practical expertise, service continuity, numbers, legitimacy, and knowledge institutions ignore
- Dependencies: time, resources, safety, internal agreement, accessible space, and networks of care
- Limits: unequal power, exhaustion, repression, scarcity, internal disagreement, and vulnerability to actors controlling necessities
- Knowledge habits: lived experience, shift records, neighborhood channels, oral memory, logs, and comparisons across repeated harm
- Local instantiation: at least two groups with different interests; define what each can refuse and accomplish without the crew
- Hooks: utility committee, tenant action, clinic shortage, unsafe job, missing worker, community defense, or media campaign
- Caution: never turn a community into a reward, “territory,” helpless client, or moral monolith
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-everyday-life`, `claim-cyberpunk-housing-work`, `claim-cyberpunk-infrastructure-rebuild`.

### `cyberpunk-faction-media-culture` — Media And Cultural Network

- Status: broad cross-era model; technology and named outlets are era-specific
- Public mask: journalism, entertainment, art, music, braindance where supported, advertising, community reporting, or political messaging
- Desire: reveal, persuade, perform, survive, profit, mobilize, preserve memory, control a narrative, or protect a source
- Methods: investigation, performance, recording, editing, broadcast, rumor, protest, patronage, leaks, and audience organization
- Capabilities: attention, source networks, public memory, cultural legitimacy, production skill, and the ability to connect separate harms
- Dependencies: credibility, audience, distribution, power, equipment, sources, venue access, and protection
- Limits: censorship, ownership, retaliation, misinformation, scarce reach, audience disagreement, and the danger imposed on sources
- Knowledge habits: recordings, source protection, editorial judgment, analytics, archives, community testimony, and contested framing
- Local instantiation: one channel, outlet, performer network, or movement with an audience and distribution path
- Hooks: suppressed casualty story, stolen recording, exploitative contract, fake attribution, missing source, or concert as organizing space
- Caution: publishing is not automatic truth, safety, reach, or political victory
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-media-culture`, `claim-cyberpunk-2077-social-world`.

### `cyberpunk-faction-net-technical` — Net And Technical Actors

- Status: era-specific model
- Public mask: Netrunner, Tech, administrator, infrastructure worker, security engineer, repair collective, data broker, or ordinary operator
- Desire: maintain a system, gain access, protect clients, learn, repair, expose, profit, or preserve autonomy
- Methods: physical service, direct connection, code, architecture design, device access, data analysis, social engineering, and era-supported quickhacks
- Capabilities: bounded to a named system, tool set, access path, and selected source
- Dependencies: hardware, power, physical reach, credentials, clients, maintenance, operational security, and accurate system knowledge
- Limits: isolation, countermeasures, damaged records, human operators, trace, time, and the fact that not every device is a NET Architecture
- Knowledge habits: logs, maps, code, diagnostics, backups, peer exchange, and incomplete technical documentation
- Local instantiation: define one system and one custodian before defining the intrusion
- Hooks: orphaned Architecture, failed CitiNet node, tampered Agent, copied access credential, hostile quickhack, or repair that reveals hidden custody
- Caution: no magic hacking, universal device control, or cross-era capability
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-net-2045`, `claim-cyberpunk-net-2070`.

### `cyberpunk-faction-state` — State And Interstate Actors

- Status: source-gated; broad examples include Night City, Northern California/Free State, NUSA, and specialized authorities
- Public mask: law, defense, citizenship, public service, border control, diplomacy, emergency management, or national security
- Desire: preserve jurisdiction, legitimacy, security, revenue, territory, intelligence, and policy goals
- Methods: law, permits, records, courts, policing, military force, intelligence, contracts, sanctions, and negotiation
- Capabilities: formal authority, identity systems, armed force, infrastructure, detention, diplomacy, and public resources
- Dependencies: personnel, logistics, political support, inter-agency cooperation, information, corporate relationships, and compliance
- Limits: borders, rival jurisdictions, budgets, secrecy, public legitimacy, internal conflict, and incomplete local control
- Knowledge habits: compartmentalized files, classified reporting, public records, diplomatic channels, and competing agency assessments
- Local instantiation: one office, unit, mission, or official; never “the government” as a single actor
- Hooks: jurisdiction collision, border hold, covert operation, presidential or executive interest, identity error, or corporate exemption
- Caution: exact NUSA/Free State relations and Dogtown politics require selected-era research
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-2045-governance`, `claim-cyberpunk-dogtown-nusa`, `claim-cyberpunk-global-scope-limited`.

## Opening Power Budget

For `cyberpunk-frame-last-light`, instantiate only:

1. one community or labor network that depends on the failing service
2. one district authority, contractor, corporation, or security claimant
3. one Fixer, supplier, or Nomad transport unit connecting the missing component
4. one technical custodian or worker who understands the physical system

A gang, criminal organization, Media, Netrunner, or additional corporation may begin as a clock or contact rather than a full faction. Famous organizations need not appear at all; an original local contractor can carry the pressure.

## Power Clock Contract

Every active power records:

- selected era
- desired end state
- next action if ignored
- local face and decision authority
- capability available now
- contract, legitimacy, or story used
- structural dependency
- hard limit
- internal disagreement
- evidence or rumor relied upon
- information missing
- people who pay the cost
- condition that changes the plan

“Arasaka,” “Militech,” “the government,” “the gangs,” “the Nomads,” or “the community” never advances a clock without a local actor and route of action.
