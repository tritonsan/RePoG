# Cyberpunk Universe Regions And Operational Theaters

## Use Contract

These cards are selectable theaters, not active places and not an exhaustive world atlas. Materialize one physical opening theater plus at most one connection layer. Scores compare campaign utility from 1 (low) to 5 (high); they are RePoG-original design judgments, not canon ratings.

Canonical names establish broad geography. Exact streets, organizations, demographics, and conditions require the selected era source. Package-original places must not replace named districts or secretly cause published events.

## Physical Theater Cards

### `cyberpunk-region-rebuilding-district` — Rebuilding District

- Status: default campaign-instance canvas for 2045
- Promise: one lived district where reconstruction, displacement, services, commerce, and competing protection claims meet
- Daily rhythm: shift changes, generator schedules, water queues, deliveries, repairs, school or care routines, market setup, entertainment, patrols, and CitiNet outages
- Essential systems: one power/water junction, one clinic or medic route, one housing cluster, one transit link, one local market, and one communications node
- Active interests: residents, workers, a district representative, a contractor or corporation, a security provider, a Fixer, and a transporter
- Pressures: scarce parts, contested land or records, unstable utilities, unsafe work, protection demands, displacement, and a deadline no firefight can repair
- Affordances: mixed-crew play, negotiation, repair, investigation, escort, media, medicine, defense, and visible consequences
- Dangers: treating poverty as scenery, making locals helpless, or assuming reconstruction belongs to whoever brings guns
- Player-known account: the district is functioning because people keep it functioning, and every claimant needs at least one thing it cannot provide alone.
- Expansion trigger: an active supply route, contract, signal, or resident relationship requires one connected theater
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-2045-governance`, `claim-cyberpunk-infrastructure-rebuild`, `claim-cyberpunk-everyday-life`.

### `cyberpunk-region-port-industrial` — Port And Industrial Corridor

- Status: 2045 logistics theater
- Promise: factories, warehouses, docks, Nomad depots, contractors, and worker communities turn every cargo decision into district politics
- Daily rhythm: manifests, loading, fabrication, repair, shift labor, inspections, security handoffs, informal trade, and hazardous cleanup
- Essential systems: port or freight access, warehouse custody, fuel, fabrication capacity, worker transport, and communications
- Active interests: dock or factory workers, Nomad carriers, corporate tenants, criminal brokers, customs-like authorities, security contractors, and local residents
- Pressures: duplicate cargo claims, strike or lockout, unsafe storage, toxic exposure, sabotage allegations, missing workers, and route competition
- Affordances: convoy escort, investigation, labor alliance, heist, extraction, Tech work, Fixer negotiation, and cross-district supply play
- Dangers: making workers disposable, using trafficking as casual color, or assuming every manifest error is a conspiracy
- Player-known account: movement creates value because specific people load, repair, record, guard, and receive the goods.
- Expansion trigger: follow one shipment or worker rather than opening the entire port
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-nomad-logistics`, `claim-cyberpunk-scarcity-economy`, `claim-cyberpunk-district-variation`.

### `cyberpunk-region-night-market` — Night Market And Service Corridor

- Status: 2045 social/logistics theater
- Promise: a temporary or recurring market links Fixers, makers, medics, performers, clients, security, stolen goods, legitimate surplus, and scarce necessities
- Daily rhythm: sourcing, verification, setup, reputation checks, bargaining, repair, food service, entertainment, cleanup, and relocation
- Essential systems: invitation or access network, storage, power, transport, medical backup, trusted appraisal, and security settlement
- Active interests: one or more Fixers, vendors, neighborhood hosts, Nomad suppliers, corporate buyers, counterfeiters, performers, and customers
- Pressures: contaminated or defective stock, exclusivity demands, copied invitations, extortion, provenance disputes, urgent community need, and a supplier who cannot arrive
- Affordances: brokerage, social play, Tech/Medtech work, investigation, Night Market operation, reputation, and noncombat solutions
- Dangers: treating the market as a universal item menu or every vendor as criminal
- Player-known account: trust, route access, and verification matter as much as Eurobucks.
- Expansion trigger: a selected item's actual origin or destination becomes active
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-night-markets-fixers`, `claim-cyberpunk-scarcity-economy`, `claim-cyberpunk-housing-work`.

### `cyberpunk-region-zone-edge` — Combat Zone And Hot Zone Edge

- Status: 2045 high-risk theater; exact canonical placement requires source review
- Promise: people live, salvage, trade, hide, and organize where security and utilities are least reliable
- Daily rhythm: scavenging, radiation or hazard checks where applicable, repair, water and power improvisation, gang patrols, mutual aid, market exchange, and movement before conditions change
- Essential systems: safe routes, hazard knowledge, protective equipment, salvage custody, community warning, and extraction capacity
- Active interests: residents, scavengers, gangs, criminal organizations, security incursions, claim jumpers, contractors, and displaced communities
- Pressures: dangerous salvage, false hazard maps, redevelopment claims, missing crews, contaminated goods, and outsiders who call inhabited space empty
- Affordances: survival, salvage, rescue, community defense, historical evidence, and hard choices about extraction versus rebuilding
- Dangers: post-apocalyptic tourism, erasing residents, consequence-free contamination, or equating low security with no social order
- Player-known account: official abandonment does not mean empty, ownerless, or unknowable.
- Expansion trigger: a recovered object connects to one living claimant or dated event
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-war-datakrash`, `claim-cyberpunk-night-city-history`, `claim-cyberpunk-district-variation`.

### `cyberpunk-region-civic-executive` — Civic And Executive Boundary

- Status: 2045 institutional theater
- Promise: district government, corporate enclaves, utilities, courts, security contracts, luxury, and servant labor touch at controlled boundaries
- Daily rhythm: permits, council work, procurement, maintenance, deliveries, screenings, domestic labor, lobbying, emergency response, and data retention
- Essential systems: credentials, substations, transport, records, guarded access, service labor, and communications
- Active interests: district managers, council representatives, corporations, private security, NCPD-like authorities, contractors, residents, servants, and media
- Pressures: procurement corruption, unequal response, utility diversion, identity exclusion, corporate rivalry, whistleblowing, and a protected zone dependent on neglected infrastructure
- Affordances: corporate intrigue, civic investigation, internal affairs, social infiltration, data custody, and visible class conflict
- Dangers: assuming elite space is self-sufficient, government is unified, or one executive controls every contractor
- Player-known account: walls move risk outward; they do not eliminate dependence.
- Expansion trigger: identify one contract, office, substation, or decision-maker
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-2045-governance`, `claim-cyberpunk-identities-citizenship`, `claim-cyberpunk-corporate-change`.

### `cyberpunk-region-entertainment-media` — Entertainment And Media District

- Status: usable in 2045 or a selected 2070s mode; technology must be era-correct
- Promise: performance, braindance where era-supported, nightlife, journalism, advertising, tourism, gangs, and corporate messaging compete for attention
- Daily rhythm: bookings, editing, rehearsals, broadcast, moderation, door work, transport, harm reduction, fan communities, cleanup, and rumor circulation
- Essential systems: venue labor, production equipment, rights or patronage, power, communications, security, and distribution
- Active interests: performers, Rockerboys, Medias, workers, venue operators, gangs, sponsors, Fixers, fans, and independent channels
- Pressures: suppressed reporting, exploitative contracts, stolen recordings, unsafe venues, manufactured scandal, radicalization, and ownership of a person's image or experience
- Affordances: Media investigation, Rockerboy movement, social influence, protection, covert recording, and public accountability
- Dangers: sexualizing every venue, erasing workers, treating audiences as gullible, or making attention equal truth
- Player-known account: a story changes power only through reach, credibility, timing, and people willing to act on it.
- Expansion trigger: follow an actual source, audience, sponsor, or distribution channel
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-media-culture`, `claim-cyberpunk-everyday-life`, `claim-cyberpunk-2077-social-world`.

### `cyberpunk-region-badlands-route` — Badlands And Nomad Route

- Status: mobile 2045 or era-selected theater
- Promise: convoys, depots, damaged infrastructure, settlements, weather, fuel, repair, and family obligations make mobility a social system
- Daily rhythm: route checks, maintenance, driving, trading, escort, camp work, communications, care, scouting, and negotiation for passage
- Essential systems: vehicles, fuel, parts, drivers, route knowledge, safe stops, communications, and reciprocal relationships
- Active interests: one Nomad family or nation unit, settlements, corporate shippers, smugglers, rival carriers, border authorities, raiders, and travelers
- Pressures: route closure, damaged bridge, disputed cargo, family disagreement, security contract, stranded people, and city clients who misunderstand convoy obligations
- Affordances: courier work, convoy campaign, exploration, rescue, trade, vehicle action, rebuilding, and mobile community play
- Dangers: “road warrior” stereotype, treating land as empty, flattening all Nomads into one culture, or making smuggling their only livelihood
- Player-known account: transport power rests on maintenance, trust, route knowledge, and people who can choose not to carry a client.
- Expansion trigger: one route endpoint becomes a physical theater
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-nomad-logistics`, `claim-cyberpunk-corporate-change`.

### `cyberpunk-region-free-state-border` — Free State And NUSA Border Route

- Status: source-gated political/logistics theater
- Promise: legal identity, customs, corporate contracts, military or police authority, smuggling, migration, and competing state narratives meet along a route
- Daily rhythm: inspections, permits, cargo transfer, patrols, waiting, bribery, legal aid, maintenance, intelligence gathering, and family travel
- Essential systems: recognized documents, checkpoints, communications, transport, holding space, legal or illicit brokers, and safe onward routes
- Active interests: Free State authorities, NUSA-linked actors where era-supported, corporations, Nomad carriers, travelers, local communities, security, and media
- Pressures: identity mismatch, jurisdictional dispute, intelligence operation, blocked medicine, military escalation, refugee harm, and a corporate exemption
- Affordances: political intrigue, courier work, extraction, investigation, diplomacy, and competing legitimacy
- Dangers: invented geopolitics, dehumanizing migrants, making every official corrupt, or treating a border as only an obstacle course
- Player-known account: authority changes across the line, but every side relies on people and routes that cross it.
- Expansion trigger: targeted research defines the exact year, border, agencies, and law
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-night-city-history`, `claim-cyberpunk-dogtown-nusa`, `claim-cyberpunk-global-scope-limited`.

### `cyberpunk-region-dogtown` — Dogtown Political Enclave

- Status: 2077/*Phantom Liberty* opt-in only
- Promise: a dangerous district supports espionage, political intrigue, fractured loyalties, and survival under contested authority
- Daily rhythm: source-specific and campaign-instance; do not infer it from 2045
- Essential systems: selected 2077 source must define access, security, markets, communications, and political actors
- Active interests: Dogtown-local powers, NUS actors, agents, residents, merchants, mercenaries, and information brokers
- Pressures: extraction, divided loyalty, political violence, surveillance, cover identities, and bargains that shift risk onto residents
- Affordances: spy thriller, political team, defector extraction, counterintelligence, and high-stakes negotiation
- Dangers: importing V's role, spoiling *Phantom Liberty*, treating Dogtown as an empty combat map, or inventing unsupported officeholders
- Player-known account: only the expansion's broad premise is safe before a spoiler contract.
- Expansion trigger: group accepts 2077 technology, protagonist proximity, and *Phantom Liberty* spoiler bands
- Source claim: `claim-cyberpunk-dogtown-nusa`.

### `cyberpunk-region-global-orbital` — Global Or Orbital Theater

- Status: source-gated; not a ready default region
- Promise: international corporate routes, distant cities, oceans, launch infrastructure, orbital interests, and Highrider questions can widen the universe beyond Night City
- Daily rhythm: unset until a named location and source are selected
- Essential systems: transport, life support where relevant, jurisdiction, labor, communications, supply, and emergency capacity
- Active interests: must come from the selected source rather than generic “global corporations”
- Pressures: isolation, supply dependency, jurisdiction, labor, evacuation, communications delay, and corporate/state competition
- Affordances: international operation, orbital campaign, diplomacy, specialized logistics, and a radically different social scale
- Dangers: treating Earth outside Night City as homogeneous, inventing cultures, or using orbital communities as a prestige backdrop
- Player-known account: the universe extends beyond Night City, but this package does not pretend to contain that atlas.
- Expansion trigger: targeted source and cultural pass creates a separate bounded operating model
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-global-scope-limited`, `claim-cyberpunk-night-city-history`.

## Connection Layers

### `cyberpunk-region-citinet-layer` — 2045 CitiNet And Local Architecture

- Status: non-geographic 2045 connection layer
- Promise: local services, Agents, Data Pool use, isolated NET Architectures, physical systems, and human operators connect places without restoring a seamless old global NET
- Use: pair with one physical theater only
- Required record: device or service, access path, controller, data, copies, local Architecture if any, physical endpoint, and intrusion trace
- Dangers: magic hacking, citywide remote control, treating an Agent as a Netrunnable Architecture, or importing 2070 quickhacks
- Source claims: `claim-cyberpunk-net-2045`, `claim-cyberpunk-war-datakrash`.

### `cyberpunk-region-quickhack-layer` — 2070s Quickhack Environment

- Status: non-geographic 2070s connection layer
- Promise: source-supported quickhacks and direct-connection Netrunning can make bodies and devices immediate tactical information surfaces
- Use: only with `cyberpunk-2070-edgerunners`, `cyberpunk-2077`, or a researched later-era mode
- Required record: selected rules source, target eligibility, range/access, defensive context, evidence, bodily boundary, and fictional consequence
- Dangers: importing a UI as physics, universal vulnerability, unbounded mind control, or claiming the public Mission Kit preview supplies complete extended 2070 Netrunning
- Source claim: `claim-cyberpunk-net-2070`.

## Default Opening Network

A `cyberpunk-region-rebuilding-district` materialization begins with no more than:

- one original sub-district or bounded redevelopment area
- one housing cluster
- one clinic, medic, or emergency route
- one power/water/communications dependency
- one market or local commerce node
- one workshop or repair capacity
- one transport depot or route handoff
- one community institution
- one district office, contractor, or corporate claimant
- one security provider with a limited contract
- one Fixer or broker and one transporter
- five to seven active faces total
- one physical failure deadline
- one institutional claim deadline
- one selected connection layer at most

Citywide corporate leadership, every gang, national politics, Old NET expeditions, Dogtown, orbital affairs, and famous characters remain offstage until an active route requires them.
