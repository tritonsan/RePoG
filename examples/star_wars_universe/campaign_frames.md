# Star Wars Universe Campaign Frames

## Use Contract

These six frames are RePoG-original proposals built from sourced setting constraints. They are not lost Star Wars stories, summaries of published plots, or Canon events. Materialize exactly one frame and its matching protected truth. Alternate frames and secrets remain inert.

Player-facing sections may be shown only after continuity, era, spoiler, and safety choices. Every **GM-only protected truth** and every `*-secret-*` id must remain outside player-visible material until its reveal requirements are met.

## `star-wars-frame-broken-beacon` — The Broken Beacon

### Player-Facing Promise

Keep a frontier corridor connected when its navigation beacons begin misdirecting the people who rely on medicine, food, repair, trade, and recognition.

### Visible Situation

During an exact-ABY-unspecified early New Republic reconstruction interval, three original communities share a damaged beacon chain. Some ships receive safe coordinates; others are rejected or redirected. A bounded New Republic mission calls the records corrupted. Local delegates fear compulsory remapping. Freighters cannot guarantee delivery, and a clinic has missed two transfers. Remnant raiders are rumored beyond the corridor, but no verified attack explains the pattern.

### Active Actors

- `star-wars-faction-reconstruction-mission`
- `star-wars-faction-local-council`
- `star-wars-faction-freight-cooperative`
- `star-wars-faction-aid-network`

### Causal Chain

`restored official beacon registry → hidden wartime routes conflict with authenticated records → selective redirection → missed aid and cargo → emergency controls → protected communities face exposure or isolation`

### Shared Clocks

This frame uses exactly two package-original, player-visible clocks defined in [World Operating Model](world_operating_model.md):

- `star-wars-clock-corridor-isolation`
- `star-wars-clock-emergency-jurisdiction`

All four powers feed these clocks. They receive no faction clocks. The protected truth is not written on either clock.

### Neutral Action Space

Escort essential ships, compare nav logs, inspect beacons, share coordinates under conditions, interview route workers, map failures, repair hardware, negotiate a protected registry, establish manual couriers, challenge seizure, investigate remnant capability, or build a consensual fallback.

### Inaction Trajectory

1. **Isolation 1/4:** routine deliveries and transfers become predictably late.
2. **Jurisdiction 1/4:** mandatory transponder checks begin.
3. **Isolation 2/4 and Jurisdiction 2/4:** a medical or food transit fails; permits narrow.
4. **Isolation 3/4 and Jurisdiction 3/4:** one settlement loses reliable passage while records or equipment enter mission control.
5. **Isolation 4/4 or Jurisdiction 4/4:** dangerous unsurveyed travel, corridor closure, compulsory mapping, or seizure becomes imminent if the relevant owner has means and evidence.

These are possible evidence-backed developments, not automatic scenes.

### PC Prompts

- Who depends on your crew reaching one specific destination?
- Which authority has an incomplete or dangerous record about your community?
- What practical skill do you contribute when a blaster or the Force cannot solve the problem?
- What route would you refuse to reveal, and to whom?

### GM-Only Protected Truth — `star-wars-frame-broken-beacon-secret-001`

During Imperial occupation, a local routing collective altered beacon records and maintained shadow routes to divert inspections away from settlements sheltering refugees, deserters, dissidents, and unregistered droids. The protection was distributed across ordinary maintenance changes, not one master sabotage device. New Republic restoration software authenticates old Imperial records while the aging shadow network still tries to keep marked routes hidden. Destroying it exposes communities to remnant raiders and hostile claimants; preserving it unchanged leaves emergency travel unsafe and newcomers excluded. A fair repair requires corroborated evidence, consent-based mapping, amnesty or accountable refusal, and replacement safety arrangements.

### Reveal Requirements

Reveal only after the PCs establish three independent elements: a repeatable redirection pattern, testimony from an affected protected community, and surviving maintenance or route records. A collaborator's identity requires consent or direct evidence.

### Safe Foreshadowing

- failures cluster around records created or altered during occupation
- many redirections move ships away from known inspection approaches
- older local maps contain destinations absent from the restored registry
- beacon maintenance signatures show coordinated work by several hands

### Forbidden Early Wording

Do not call the network a Sith trap, sentient beacon, terrorist sabotage, Republic conspiracy, or remnant attack before evidence supports it. Do not expose hidden settlements or collaborators through GM narration.

### Boundaries And Expansion

No famous rescuer, sector-wide fleet battle, or universal New Republic conspiracy. Expand beyond three systems only through player-created connection. Source anchors: `claim-star-wars-new-republic`, `claim-star-wars-hyperspace`, `claim-star-wars-refugees`.

## `star-wars-frame-empty-seat` — The Empty Seat

### Player-Facing Promise

Keep a civic compact from collapsing when one community's delegate disappears and two valid records name different successors.

### Visible Situation

On an original civic world in a selected Republic or New Republic interval, a route-and-relief assembly must allocate scarce transport capacity. One district's delegate misses the deciding session. The public registry names a cautious administrator as successor; a sealed courier record names a worker organizer. Both records authenticate. Outside officials threaten to suspend funding until representation is clear, while residents insist neither outsider understands how the district survived the prior regime.

### Active Actors

- `star-wars-faction-local-council`
- `star-wars-faction-reconstruction-mission` or era-appropriate Republic office
- `star-wars-faction-freight-cooperative`
- a bounded civic coalition created in Session 0

### Causal Chain

`occupation-era representation workaround → transition restores conflicting records → delegate absence → allocation freezes → outside deadline → district loses voice or compact legitimacy`

### Neutral Action Space

Locate the delegate, authenticate custody chains, hold listening sessions, protect witnesses, negotiate interim representation, separate emergency service from political recognition, inspect courier logs, challenge the funding deadline, or design a new selection process.

### Inaction Trajectory

1. Essential cargo allocation is delayed.
2. Rivals publish incompatible claims of fraud.
3. Outside funding enters administrative hold.
4. Security removes residents from the assembly chamber.
5. The compact either excludes the district or loses external recognition.

### PC Prompts

- Which community trusted you with a message it could not safely publish?
- What does legitimate representation require beyond a correct record?
- Which urgent service should continue even during a political dispute?
- When have you benefited from a procedure that harmed someone else?

### GM-Only Protected Truth — `star-wars-frame-empty-seat-secret-001`

The missing delegate is alive and voluntarily hidden. Under occupation, the district used two linked offices: one public intermediary acceptable to authorities and one internally selected organizer who controlled actual service decisions. Both succession records are genuine because each protects a different function. The delegate disappeared after an outside auditor demanded one authoritative list that would expose former resistance households. Neither successor alone carries legitimate consent for both roles. Resolution requires safe contact, separation of service and diplomatic functions, and a new public mandate rather than proving one record false.

### Reveal Requirements

Require both custody chains, testimony from district residents across the two networks, and a consent-safe message from the delegate or direct evidence of their purpose. Authentication alone is insufficient.

### Safe Foreshadowing

- both records route through real offices with different audiences
- prior decisions carry two distinct approval marks
- residents describe representation as a pair of responsibilities
- the delegate took no funds and left planned service instructions

### Forbidden Early Wording

Do not call the delegate kidnapped, corrupt, assassinated, or a traitor. Do not frame one successor as the “real voice” before community consent is established.

### Boundaries And Expansion

Keep one assembly, district, and allocation decision. Source exact Senate or New Republic procedure before invoking it. Source anchors: `claim-star-wars-senate`, `claim-star-wars-new-republic`, `claim-star-wars-comms`.

## `star-wars-frame-unmarked-cargo` — The Unmarked Cargo

### Player-Facing Promise

Deliver urgent medical cargo through a tightening port inspection without deciding too quickly which passengers count as property, contraband, or people.

### Visible Situation

A small freighter accepts sealed relief containers for an original settlement. At a Mid Rim junction or shadowport, the cargo transponder reports medical equipment while mass and power signatures suggest active droid systems. The sponsor demands speed and refuses inspection. A broker offers a forged lane permit. Port authorities warn that a theft claim has been filed against the shipment. Patients will lose treatment if the crew abandons it.

### Active Actors

- `star-wars-faction-freight-cooperative`
- `star-wars-faction-aid-network`
- `star-wars-faction-droid-network`
- one bounded port authority, guild broker, or era-appropriate patrol

### Causal Chain

`droid seizure and threatened wipes → aid worker hides memory-bearing units in medical shipment → false manifest triggers inspection → patients and droids share one route → property claim and deadline collide`

### Neutral Action Space

Inspect by consent, verify patient need, speak with the droids, find replacement equipment, challenge the property claim, negotiate protected passage, split cargo safely, expose the sponsor, accept smuggling risk, create an escrow arrangement, or reroute under escort.

### Inaction Trajectory

1. The port impounds the freighter.
2. Patients lose scheduled treatment capacity.
3. The claimant requests remote shutdown and memory access.
4. A syndicate offers passage in exchange for ownership debt.
5. The droids are seized or the settlement is publicly blamed for trafficking.

### PC Prompts

- What cargo have you carried without asking enough questions?
- Which machine relationship do you refuse to describe as ownership?
- Who taught you that a forged document can protect or endanger lives?
- What obligation survives if payment disappears?

### GM-Only Protected Truth — `star-wars-frame-unmarked-cargo-secret-001`

The containers hold both genuine medical systems and three droids whose prior controller ordered memory wipes after they witnessed forced labor. The droids chose to leave and agreed to travel hidden, but the aid sponsor falsified the entire manifest without telling the settlement or crew. One droid carries calibration knowledge needed by the medical equipment; none is merely a component. The theft claimant wants the witnesses and their memories, not the hardware's market value. A fair outcome requires hearing each droid, protecting patients, contesting coercive custody, and addressing the sponsor's dangerous unilateral choice.

### Reveal Requirements

Require a consent-based container inspection, direct communication with each droid, and corroboration of the wipe order or forced-labor evidence. A power signature does not prove personhood or theft by itself.

### Safe Foreshadowing

- internal power use follows distinct maintenance and communication cycles
- the theft claim demands memory integrity rather than serial-number return
- medical seals are valid but the cargo description was recently replaced
- one container responds to questions rather than diagnostic pings

### Forbidden Early Wording

Do not call the droids stolen equipment, emancipated persons, deceptive machines, or identical victims before their individual positions are known. Do not make a memory wipe comic.

### Boundaries And Expansion

Define local droid and cargo law as campaign-instance; do not claim a galactic standard. Source anchors: `claim-star-wars-droids`, `claim-star-wars-medicine`, `claim-star-wars-economy`.

## `star-wars-frame-last-salvage` — The Last Salvage Claim

### Player-Facing Promise

Recover life-support parts from a war wreck while workers, a distant claimant, and a living settlement argue over what the wreck has become.

### Visible Situation

An original settlement survives by repairing systems from a grounded military-industrial platform. A newly recognized owner presents authentic title to the hull and announces total removal. Salvagers say the platform has supplied power, shelter, and water processing for years. A damaged reactor-control module could restore the settlement or fetch enough credits to save the claimant's failing transport company. Hazard warnings suggest the next recovery may destabilize the structure.

### Active Actors

- `star-wars-faction-salvage-network`
- `star-wars-faction-local-council`
- `star-wars-faction-freight-cooperative`
- one bounded claimant, insurer, or reconstruction office

### Causal Chain

`abandoned war asset → community labor converts wreck into infrastructure → old title resurfaces → high-value component becomes scarce → removal threatens settlement → custody conflict escalates`

### Neutral Action Space

Trace title and abandonment, map community labor, assess hazards, find substitute parts, negotiate stewardship, separate movable property from embedded infrastructure, compensate the claimant, document war damage, organize workers, or halt unsafe recovery.

### Inaction Trajectory

1. Claim agents mark equipment for removal.
2. Salvagers block access and lose outside contracts.
3. Water or power rationing begins.
4. Armed security arrives or workers attempt a dangerous extraction.
5. The platform fails, is stripped, or becomes the center of a violent seizure.

### PC Prompts

- What have you repaired so thoroughly that ownership changed in your eyes?
- Which unsafe shortcut once kept your community alive?
- Whose labor is missing from the official ledger?
- What would you return even if you could keep it?

### GM-Only Protected Truth — `star-wars-frame-last-salvage-secret-001`

The claimant's title is genuine, but an occupation-era manager deliberately marked the platform unrecoverable and fled after workers disabled a weapon-control bus. The settlement later rebuilt life-support around that disabled bus; removing the reactor module as designed will reactivate a dormant targeting link and destabilize the water system. A maintenance ledger proves community labor created the current utility, while the claimant inherited debt without knowing the abandonment history. Resolution requires technical separation, recognition of transformed use, and negotiated value—not declaring either side a thief.

### Reveal Requirements

Require title provenance, structural diagnostics, and the worker maintenance ledger. The weapon link must be physically confirmed before anyone declares the platform armed.

### Safe Foreshadowing

- utility cables cross original military boundaries
- the official recovery diagram predates community modifications
- abandonment records use an unusual hazard code
- the claimant's team lacks current structural knowledge

### Forbidden Early Wording

Do not call the workers squatters, the claimant a war profiteer, the wreck a secret superweapon, or the reactor a simple treasure.

### Boundaries And Expansion

Keep one platform, settlement, claimant, and component. Source exact salvage law only if a selected work supplies it. Source anchors: `claim-star-wars-ferrix`, `claim-star-wars-starships`, `claim-star-wars-economy`.

## `star-wars-frame-names-in-armor` — Names in the Armor

### Player-Facing Promise

Keep a Clone Wars support unit alive and morally legible when a readiness order treats wounded, exhausted, and dissenting soldiers as replaceable inventory.

### Visible Situation

In a selected, source-researched interval of `star-wars-era-fall-jedi`, one original support outpost receives orders to return every available clone to combat duty. Medical files show many are unfit. The roster lists several troopers as transferred, but their bunks and chosen names remain in use. A Republic inspector suspects desertion or supply fraud. Local civilians depend on the same evacuation craft the order would requisition.

### Active Actors

- one original clone support unit based on `star-wars-faction-patrol`
- `star-wars-faction-aid-network`
- `star-wars-faction-local-council`
- one bounded Republic command or inspection team

### Causal Chain

`casualty pressure → quartermaster creates protective transfer records → readiness order reconciles databases → wounded clones face redeployment → civilian evacuation loses transport → inspection threatens collective punishment`

### Neutral Action Space

Interview troopers individually, audit readiness, document injuries with consent, challenge the order, repair alternate transport, negotiate medical status, protect civilians, expose supply manipulation, arrange conscientious transfer, or accept a bounded defensive mission.

### Inaction Trajectory

1. Medical exemptions are suspended.
2. Evacuation craft enter military custody.
3. Troopers are separated and questioned.
4. Civilians lose their planned passage.
5. The unit deploys unfit, resists openly, or becomes a target for discipline.

### PC Prompts

- Which name did you choose, and who uses it correctly?
- What order would you refuse even if refusal endangered your place in the unit?
- Which civilian relationship changed how you understand duty?
- What care do you need that command calls inefficiency?

### GM-Only Protected Truth — `star-wars-frame-names-in-armor-secret-001`

The quartermaster and medic created false transfer entries with the individual consent of injured troopers so they could recover and keep an evacuation route available. They did not sell supplies or conceal enemy agents. One listed trooper chose to leave military life; another wants to return when fit; a third has not decided. The inspector's database reconciliation will expose all three and cancel civilian passage, but the inspector does not know the medical records were previously altered under pressure from a commander seeking impossible readiness numbers. Resolution requires individual clone agency, protected medical truth, command accountability, and civilian transport—not a single answer for the unit.

### Reveal Requirements

Require consent from affected clone individuals, medical evidence with privacy controls, and independent proof of the prior readiness pressure. Do not reveal a deserter's location through omniscient narration.

### Safe Foreshadowing

- transfer entries were created in separate conversations
- armor names remain while numerical inventory records change
- medical supply use matches real treatment
- the evacuation craft's maintenance schedule predates the new order

### Forbidden Early Wording

Do not call clones identical, programmed to obey, defective, children as an insult, or disposable. Do not use Order 66 as a surprise if it exceeds the accepted spoiler and safety scope.

### Boundaries And Expansion

Choose exact Clone Wars date, command structure, known orders, and clone safeguards before play. Avoid replacing published battles. Source anchors: `claim-star-wars-clones`, `claim-star-wars-republic`, `claim-star-wars-jedi`.

## `star-wars-frame-quiet-current` — The Quiet Current

### Player-Facing Promise

Protect a place of unusual perception when pilgrims, researchers, residents, and a frightened authority all claim to know what the Force requires.

### Visible Situation

At an original or explicitly selected Force site, travelers report overlapping dreams and moments of shared direction after a hyperspace disturbance. A local stewardship circle limits access. Researchers want unrestricted instruments. An authority fears a hidden Sith device; opportunists sell false relics. Nearby residents report that animal migration and power systems changed before the visions began.

### Active Actors

- `star-wars-faction-force-community`
- `star-wars-faction-local-council`
- one research, archive, or era-appropriate Jedi-adjacent group
- one bounded security or reconstruction office

### Causal Chain

`environmental and route disturbance → site phenomena intensify → traditions interpret differently → access rush disrupts stewardship → fear labels site dangerous → seizure or exploitation threatens residents`

### Neutral Action Space

Listen without assuming, map experiences with consent, compare sensor and ecological evidence, establish access rules, test noninvasively, remove false relics, mediate traditions, redirect pilgrims, challenge seizure, or leave part of the phenomenon unresolved.

### Inaction Trajectory

1. Crowds disrupt local life and habitat.
2. Conflicting interpretations become accusations.
3. Security quarantines the site.
4. Researchers or looters remove material.
5. Residents lose access, or a violent confrontation fixes one interpretation by force.

### PC Prompts

- What does the Force mean to you, if anything?
- Which experience would you keep private even from a trusted teacher?
- Who has the right to say no to study or pilgrimage?
- What evidence could change your interpretation without erasing it?

### GM-Only Protected Truth — `star-wars-frame-quiet-current-secret-001`

The stewardship circle concealed an old route-observation chamber after a prior regime exploited local sensitives as navigational instruments. Its instruments now resonate with a changed hyperspace mass pattern, while living migration through the site shapes how different visitors experience the disturbance. A caretaker narrowed access to prevent renewed coercion but did not cause the phenomenon and does not fully understand it. The site may genuinely involve the Force; the frame does not reduce it to machinery. Resolution requires resident consent, protection from exploitation, plural interpretation, and safe navigation research.

### Reveal Requirements

Require ecological observation, instrument records, testimony from affected visitors with consent, and the caretaker's account or equivalent direct evidence. No single vision proves the whole explanation.

### Safe Foreshadowing

- experiences differ by relationship and expectation rather than moral alignment
- sensor changes precede the pilgrimage rush
- local migrations overlap old observation markings
- the caretaker warns about exploitation more than darkness

### Forbidden Early Wording

Do not call the site Sith, Jedi, sentient, corrupting, a chosen-one test, or merely technological. Do not reveal a PC's sensitivity or belief without player authority.

### Boundaries And Expansion

Select one site and no more than two traditions. Exact Force abilities and history require sources. Source anchors: `claim-star-wars-force`, `claim-star-wars-force-traditions`, `claim-star-wars-hyperspace`.

## Materialization Gate

Before copying a frame into active campaign files:

- [ ] continuity, era, date policy, media, works, spoilers, and rules are accepted
- [ ] one physical theater and at most one connection layer are selected
- [ ] group fantasy, mandate, size, authority, refusal, and exit are accepted
- [ ] exactly one frame is selected
- [ ] only its matching protected truth is materialized
- [ ] the complete protected truth, reveal gates, and forbidden wording exist only in the GM-only section of `campaign/knowledge_boundaries.md`, the workflow-designated authoritative owner for fact truth and reveal status
- [ ] optional clue or prep artifacts contain only the protected fact id and flexible delivery references, never a copied truth body or independent reveal status
- [ ] materialization blocks if the authoritative GM-only section is unavailable or any player-visible projection would receive protected content
- [ ] no `*-secret-*` id or protected body enters a player-visible target
- [ ] the retained clock model is capped; the default has exactly two shared clocks and zero faction clocks
- [ ] visible actors receive original local faces, constituencies, dependencies, limits, and next actions
- [ ] sensitive themes have explicit lines, veils, and handling tools
- [ ] famous-character and Force-sensitive proximity are recorded
- [ ] original places, technology, incidents, and powers are labeled
- [ ] claim ids and uncertainty survive materialization

A custom frame may replace all six but must still define a visible need, causal chain, bounded actors, neutral action space, inaction trajectory, protected truth, reveal requirements, safety boundary, and exclusive secret route.
