# Star Wars Universe Session 0 Options

## Player-Safe Use

This file contains no campaign-frame solutions. It may be shown after the group agrees that it wants to engage with the Star Wars fictional setting. Source hierarchy, protected facts, and technical materialization remain Designer/GM concerns.

## Default Pitch

The Empire has been defeated, but one declaration did not repair every route or settle every claim. During an early, exact-ABY-unspecified interval of the New Republic, an original frontier corridor links three communities to medicine, food, trade, repair, and political recognition. Its navigation beacons have begun rejecting or redirecting selected ships. You are three to five original people with a practical responsibility to keep the corridor working: perhaps pilots, mechanics, medics, couriers, traders, delegates, investigators, salvagers, refugees, droids, clones, veterans, or locally grounded Force believers. You are not replacements for famous heroes. You decide whose maps become official, which routes remain protected, and what legitimate reconstruction owes people who survived by hiding.

The date, corridor, communities, crew, beacon failure, and reconstruction conditions are original campaign proposals. Select another era or fantasy if this pitch does not fit.

## Decisions In Order

### 1. Property Engagement

Choose one:

- use the official Star Wars fictional universe with this package's source boundaries
- use a generic space-fantasy setting instead
- use a strongly divergent setting retaining selected motifs
- do not use this property

Nobody must justify declining the property.

### 2. Continuity And Editorial Treatment

- `star-wars-canon` — current coordinated Canon; default
- `star-wars-legends` — selected pre-2014 Expanded Universe/Legends works
- `star-wars-visions-selected` — one selected alternate or continuity-independent *Visions* work
- `star-wars-lego-selected` — one selected LEGO alternate work
- `star-wars-crossover-selected` — one selected promotional/crossover work
- `star-wars-campaign-divergent` — precise recorded divergence

If more than one is allowed, define crossover rules. A Canon reuse of a Legends element does not import the old work's full history.

### 3. Chronology, Era, And Spoiler Ceiling

Choose a chronology mode before selecting an era:

- `star-wars-chronology-official-era` — select one official qualitative era; default for main Canon play
- `star-wars-chronology-selected-work` — use the selected work's own chronology when it does not map cleanly to an official era
- `star-wars-chronology-remixed` — record multiple remixed eras for an alternate LEGO, crossover, or similarly explicit premise
- `star-wars-chronology-not-applicable` — the selected work has no useful in-world era mapping

An optional compatibility mapping to an official era may aid discovery, but it never establishes continuity. Legends selections use their selected-work chronology unless an official-era mapping is explicitly useful. Visions, LEGO, and crossover selections do not have to accept a Canon-oriented era.

When `star-wars-chronology-official-era` is selected, choose from the following table:

| Era | Campaign promise | Minimum suggested spoiler scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `star-wars-era-dawn-jedi` | earliest Force discovery and first-Jedi context | selected work only |
| `star-wars-era-old-republic` | ancient Republic, Jedi, Sith, or Legends SWTOR work | selected Canon/Legends works; SWTOR branches separately |
| `star-wars-era-high-republic` | exploration, expansion, institutions, and frontier pressure | selected publication outcomes |
| `star-wars-era-fall-jedi` | Republic crisis, Clone Wars, Jedi militarization, and collapse | `S2` if known outcomes matter |
| `star-wars-era-reign-empire` | occupation, survival, resistance, and Imperial consolidation | `S2`–`S3` by date |
| `star-wars-era-rebellion` | coalition resistance and Imperial defeat | `S3` |
| `star-wars-era-new-republic` | reconstruction, remnants, local legitimacy, and demilitarization | `S3`; package default; `S4` only for selected later plots |
| `star-wars-era-first-order` | New Republic vulnerability, First Order threat, and Resistance | `S5` by selected work |
| `star-wars-era-new-jedi-order` | future rebuilding after *The Rise of Skywalker* | only officially released selected material |

Set an exact date only when sources support it. Otherwise record a qualitative interval. Never infer hard era boundaries from this package.

### 4. Medium And Game Layer

List allowed films, series, novels, comics, games, Databank summaries, and official articles. For each game, choose what may enter:

- fixed narrative
- selected branch or player-variable outcome
- presentation only
- mechanics only for player-facing rules, not fiction
- excluded

HUDs, respawn, fast travel, loot, talent trees, inventory, encounter density, map compression, and player-avatar omnipotence are not default lore.

### 5. Rules And Force Posture

Choose system-neutral narration or name an owned rules system. Then decide:

- no Force-sensitive PCs, opt-in sensitivity, or Force-centered group
- belief without sensitivity, sensitivity without institutional membership, or selected tradition
- how new powers, training, visions, artifacts, and traditions are approved
- whether Jedi or Sith proximity is none, distant, bounded, or central
- how consent, mind influence, prophecy, temptation, injury, healing, and evidence work
- whether combat is rare, moderate, or central

No character is assigned sensitivity, morality, lineage, destiny, or tradition.

### 6. Theater And Scale

Choose one physical theater from [Regions](regions.md) and at most one connection layer. Default: `star-wars-region-reconstruction-corridor` plus optional `star-wars-region-hyperspace-layer`.

Choose scale:

- one ship, station, district, clinic, yard, or council
- one settlement and its immediate route
- a corridor of no more than three systems — package default maximum opening scale
- one sector
- a galactic institution or war theater

Larger scales require more sources, actors, and knowledge boundaries.

### 7. Group Fantasy And Mandate

Choose one of the sixteen rows below or combine at most two compatible fantasies. Define why the group cooperates, what it can refuse, who depends on it, what authority it lacks, and how a member can leave safely.

### 8. Famous-Character Proximity

- none; published people remain history or public names — default
- distant; one may be a leader, report, or remembered figure
- bounded contact; no more than two appear for sourced narrow reasons
- high-Canon; PCs interact with published people and outcomes after targeted research
- divergent; published roles may change after a recorded divergence

Famous ancestry, mentorship, rivalry, romance, or secret kinship is never assigned without player request.

### 9. Social And War Themes

For each topic choose foreground, background, transformed, or excluded:

- authoritarian occupation and collaboration
- rebellion, sabotage, reprisals, and civilian risk
- slavery, trafficking, debt bondage, and forced labor
- clone personhood, programming, aging, military production, child-soldier framing, and veteran life
- droid personhood, ownership, restraining devices, memory wipes, and maintenance dependence
- species/culture prejudice and essentialism
- refugees, displacement, borders, records, and housing
- genocide, planetary destruction, collective punishment, and mass casualty
- torture, interrogation, prisons, mind influence, and surveillance
- Force religion, coercion, prophecy, and moral labeling
- disability, cybernetics, injury, medical access, and cure narratives
- minors in war, resistance, training, or custody

If foregrounded, identify affected people's agency and desired focus. Harm is not decorative atmosphere.

### 10. Tone And Consequence

Choose two primary tones and one excluded tone:

- frontier community responsibility
- hopeful reconstruction
- swashbuckling adventure
- political diplomacy
- procedural investigation
- freight and repair drama
- survival under occupation
- rebellion and sacrifice
- military action
- underworld pressure
- mystical discovery
- refugee aid and care
- salvage and labor
- tragic war memory

Set consequence levels for injury, death, capture, torture, memory change, cybernetic loss, ship destruction, debt, displacement, reputation, and harm to civilians.

### 11. Safety And Authorship

Record lines, veils, pause/rewind tools, check-in method, private communication route, and debrief practice. Campaigns involving minors require age bands, adult responsibility, romance boundaries, and explicit exclusion of sexual content involving minors.

## Visible Frame Menu

- **The Broken Beacon:** keep a frontier corridor connected while safe maps, hidden routes, and emergency authority conflict.
- **The Empty Seat:** restore legitimate representation when two authentic succession records name different delegates.
- **The Unmarked Cargo:** deliver medical aid while a disputed shipment raises droid autonomy, custody, and inspection questions.
- **The Last Salvage Claim:** decide what ownership means after a community turns a war wreck into essential infrastructure.
- **Names in the Armor:** protect clone individuality, care, and civilian passage when command demands impossible readiness.
- **The Quiet Current:** steward a Force-touched place amid plural beliefs, research, fear, and local consent.

Create a custom frame if none fits. Players select visible promises, never hidden solutions.

## Accessibility And Dignity Contract

- Species, homeworld, genetics, chassis, culture, and Force relationship never determine worth or morality.
- Cybernetics and disability are not villain coding, spiritual failure, automatic cure targets, or proof of lost humanity.
- Clone characters have names, preferences, consent, relationships, and futures beyond military use.
- Droid characters or NPCs receive individual treatment; the table explicitly chooses how local law and self-understanding relate.
- Memory wiping, restraining devices, programming overrides, mind tricks, interrogation, possession, visions, and prophecy require boundaries.
- Mandalorian identity is not one creed, helmet practice, profession, or temperament.
- Slavery and forced labor are never normalized by familiar genre presentation.
- Refugees are people with goals and expertise, not cargo or tragedy props.
- A participant may reject a fictional metaphor or theme without defending the choice.

## Group Bond Prompts

Choose two or three:

- We maintain a route or service none of us can protect alone.
- One community trusted us when a major institution did not.
- Our ship, station, or yard survives through shared labor and disputed ownership.
- We carry evidence that could protect and expose different people.
- We disagree about a government or movement but share responsibility for its users.
- A prior mission harmed someone; we agreed on a concrete repair.
- Each of us knows one part of a route no central map contains.
- We wrote a refusal and exit agreement before accepting the mandate.

## Opening Materialization Gate

Do not begin play until:

- [ ] property engagement, continuity, chronology policy, official era when applicable, date policy, works, and spoilers are accepted
- [ ] game narrative, branches, mechanics, and presentation are separated
- [ ] one physical theater and at most one connection layer are selected
- [ ] one group fantasy, mandate, authority limit, refusal, and exit are accepted
- [ ] rules, combat, Force, and original-capability policies are recorded
- [ ] famous-character and famous-lineage proximity are explicit
- [ ] player-authored fields remain unanswered until players answer them
- [ ] droid, clone, species, culture, disability, war, coercion, slavery, refugee, and minor safeguards are recorded
- [ ] exactly one visible frame or custom pressure is selected
- [ ] original details are labeled and official claims retain provenance

If an item is unresolved, continue Session 0 rather than silently choosing a default.
