# Dune World Operating Model

## Use Contract

These are durable causal truths, not an active campaign state. Materialize only the truths relevant to the selected era and opening scale. Every local face, institution, and pressure must still be instantiated in campaign-owned files.

## Core Causal Chain

1. Arrakis alone supplies melange in the default era.
2. Melange supports longevity, heightened awareness, prescient capacities, and the Guild's safe navigation monopoly.
3. Dependence on spice makes a lethal ecology the economic center of the Imperium.
4. Feudal Houses, CHOAM interests, the Emperor, and the Guild compete while relying on the same system.
5. The prohibition on thinking machines concentrates power in trained humans, secret schools, institutions, and controlled information.
6. Scarce transport and guarded communication let distance, secrecy, and intermediaries matter.
7. Shields, kanly, poison, assassination, and convention redirect war into asymmetric and deniable forms without making violence humane.
8. On Arrakis, every extraction plan meets water scarcity, storms, sandworms, local knowledge, labor costs, and Fremen agency.
9. Religion and prophecy can organize resistance and meaning while institutions attempt to seed, steer, or exploit them.
10. A local decision about water, spice, shelter, evidence, or loyalty can alter House standing and eventually propagate through Imperial systems.

## Durable Truth Cards

### `dune-truth-001` — One World Is A Systemic Choke Point

- Status: template-fixed in the default era
- Truth: Arrakis is the unique source of melange, so local production failures become interstellar political events.
- Table consequence: a broken crawler, false ledger, water shortage, or smuggling route can matter without beginning at throne-room scale.
- Must not imply: that every local resident understands the full Imperial consequence or that spice value excuses harm.
- Open question: which actors can prove a shortage, and who profits from uncertainty?
- Source claims: `claim-dune-arrakis-spice`, `claim-dune-choam`, `claim-dune-play-scales`.

### `dune-truth-002` — Power Is Balanced, Not Equal

- Status: template-fixed
- Truth: the Emperor, Great Houses in the Landsraad, Spacing Guild, CHOAM interests, and specialist institutions constrain one another through different kinds of leverage.
- Table consequence: no single authority automatically resolves a crisis; jurisdiction, reputation, transport, money, and force can point in different directions.
- Must not imply: constitutional equality, modern citizenship, or a neutral rule of law.
- Open question: whose law applies at the selected place and what retaliation is credible?
- Source claims: `claim-dune-feudal-imperium`, `claim-dune-power-balance`, `claim-dune-choam`.

### `dune-truth-003` — Interstellar Mobility Is Political

- Status: template-fixed
- Truth: the Guild's transport position turns travel, scheduling, secrecy, and access into leverage.
- Table consequence: leaving a world, moving troops, protecting cargo, or obtaining an audience is an action with patrons, costs, and witnesses.
- Must not imply: a ubiquitous, cheap, independent starship culture.
- Open question: what was declared to the Guild, and what traveled under another manifest?
- Source claims: `claim-dune-guild-travel`.

### `dune-truth-004` — Human Training Replaces Forbidden Automation

- Status: template-fixed; exact doctrine is continuity-sensitive
- Truth: the post-Butlerian order proscribes thinking machines and elevates trained human capacities, bounded devices, memory disciplines, and institutional schools.
- Table consequence: Mentats, Bene Gesserit, Suk doctors, Swordmasters, spies, and other specialists hold scarce capabilities but remain people with loyalties and limits.
- Must not imply: no technology, universal technophobia, magic-equivalent powers without training, or infallible specialists.
- Open question: who trained a specialist, who pays them, and what did that institution withhold?
- Source claims: `claim-dune-limits-computers`, `claim-dune-human-schools`.

### `dune-truth-005` — Violence Is Constrained Into Dangerous Shapes

- Status: template-fixed in the default era
- Truth: shields, lasgun risk, atomics convention, kanly, poison, assassination, and political retaliation make violence tactical, intimate, deniable, or catastrophic.
- Table consequence: social and physical conflict use the same landscape of leverage; winning a duel may lose a House.
- Must not imply: rules make war safe, every opponent honors convention, or a lasgun is an ordinary answer to shields.
- Open question: who can frame the incident as lawful kanly, accident, treason, or convention breach?
- Source claims: `claim-dune-kanly-convention`, `claim-dune-shields`, `claim-dune-atomics`.

### `dune-truth-006` — Arrakis Cannot Be Reduced To A Mine

- Status: template-fixed
- Truth: spice, sandworms, desert conditions, water, and living processes form a coupled ecology that Imperial extraction only partly understands.
- Table consequence: ecological disturbance creates social and economic consequences; local knowledge can outrank expensive equipment.
- Must not imply: the ecological mechanism is common knowledge or that Fremen exist only to explain it.
- Open question: which part of the cycle is observable at the opening scale?
- Source claims: `claim-dune-worm-spice-cycle`, `claim-dune-water`, `claim-dune-arrakis-spice`.

### `dune-truth-007` — Water Is Material, Social, And Sacred

- Status: template-fixed on Arrakis
- Truth: water discipline shapes bodies, architecture, labor, hospitality, death practice, status, trust, and long-term ecological hope.
- Table consequence: a ration, seal, cistern, stillsuit, body, or promise of water has context-dependent moral and political weight.
- Must not imply: one universal custom across city, village, smuggler, and sietch life.
- Open question: who owns the water, who keeps the ledger, and who is excluded?
- Source claims: `claim-dune-water`, `claim-dune-fremen`, `claim-dune-class-daily-life`.

### `dune-truth-008` — Prescience Reveals And Entraps

- Status: template-fixed as a thematic limit; exact access is campaign-instance
- Truth: prescient vision offers possibilities and pressure rather than a neutral, exhaustive future database.
- Table consequence: visions should create costly interpretation and choices, not cancel surprise or dictate a player's action.
- Must not imply: the GM may retroactively declare any outcome foreseen, or spice exposure grants reliable prophecy to everyone.
- Open question: what remains obscured, what action narrows the future, and who benefits from belief in the vision?
- Source claims: `claim-dune-prescience`.

### `dune-truth-009` — Belief Has More Than One Cause

- Status: template-fixed
- Truth: faith, ritual, myth, propaganda, Missionaria Protectiva preparation, political charisma, memory, and material struggle can coexist.
- Table consequence: religious language can be sincere and strategically used in the same scene; believers retain agency.
- Must not imply: all religion is fake, all prophecy is true, or manipulation erases culture.
- Open question: which meaning belongs to the speaker, the community, and the institution using it?
- Source claims: `claim-dune-religion`, `claim-dune-missionaria`.

### `dune-truth-010` — Information Is A Scarce Asset

- Status: template-fixed at broad level
- Truth: intelligence travels through people, records, coded forms, couriers, institutional channels, and transport bottlenecks; secrecy is an active capability.
- Table consequence: who knows, who can authenticate, and who can safely transmit a fact matter as much as the fact itself.
- Must not imply: perfect surveillance, universal real-time communications, or automatic truth from a Mentat analysis.
- Open question: what evidence survives scrutiny by a rival House or the Landsraad?
- Source claims: `claim-dune-guild-travel`, `claim-dune-human-schools`, `claim-dune-class-daily-life`.

### `dune-truth-011` — Daily Life Depends On Status And World

- Status: template-fixed as diversity; details are campaign-instance
- Truth: noble household, trained retainer, merchant, worker, pyon villager, smuggler, pilgrim, and sietch member encounter different rights, dangers, technologies, and obligations.
- Table consequence: show the labor and local institution behind every elite asset; avoid treating the Imperium as only palaces and duels.
- Must not imply: one universal Imperial culture or a simple noble/common moral binary.
- Open question: what can this person refuse, own, conceal, or leave?
- Source claims: `claim-dune-class-daily-life`, `claim-dune-feudal-imperium`, `claim-dune-arrakeen-carthag`.

### `dune-truth-012` — Institutions Act Beyond The Scene

- Status: template-fixed
- Truth: Houses, Guild, CHOAM, Sisterhood, Imperial court, smugglers, and Fremen communities pursue goals through absent agents and delayed consequences.
- Table consequence: clocks advance from motives and capacity, not because the GM needs a twist.
- Must not imply: institutions are hive minds or every local member knows the central agenda.
- Open question: what will the institution do if the PCs never intervene?
- Source claims: `claim-dune-power-balance`, `claim-dune-house-play`, `claim-dune-play-scales`.

## Everyday Life By Social Position

| Position | Routine concerns | Common leverage | Typical constraint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Great House ruler or heir | alliances, succession, fief output, reputation, security | patronage, troops, CHOAM interests, marriage, audience | visibility, obligation, rival surveillance |
| House agent or retainer | mission, loyalty, evidence, asset access, patron expectations | training, credentials, team, equipment, secrets | divided orders, deniability, expendability |
| Specialist graduate | professional doctrine, contract, reputation, institutional tie | scarce expertise, trusted access | conditioning, oath, sponsor, narrow role expectations |
| Merchant or CHOAM functionary | contracts, transport, quota, credit, authentication | manifests, shares, price knowledge, contacts | House jurisdiction, Guild access, political exposure |
| Arrakis city worker | water, heat, housing, employment, policing, ration reliability | local knowledge, labor solidarity, routine access | precarity, status, surveillance, environmental danger |
| Pyon villager or concession laborer | subsistence, water allotment, protection, local debt | community memory, terrain, indispensable labor | coercive authority, distance, scarce exit |
| Smuggler | route secrecy, buyers, crews, bribes, desert survival | off-ledger movement, information, flexible alliances | betrayal, weather, Guild/House pressure |
| Fremen sietch member | community survival, water discipline, kin, desert movement, long project | ecological knowledge, mobility, solidarity, secrecy | exposure, Imperial violence, internal obligations |
| Imperial court participant | access, ritual, favor, reputation, factional signals | proximity, etiquette, secrets, sponsorship | scrutiny, dependency, sudden disgrace |

These categories are prompts, not fixed identities. A campaign must create specific local institutions and ask players how their characters belong.

| Ornithopters | fast atmospheric travel, scouting, rescue, pursuit | weather, maintenance, visibility, pilot skill, desert landing risk |
| Harvesters and carryalls | industrial spice extraction and emergency lift | wormsign, coordination, sabotage, quota pressure, equipment failure |

| Bene Gesserit disciplines | observation, bodily control, influence, memory traditions | training, context, institutional agenda, resistance, consequence |

## Conflict Model

Dune conflict is not divided cleanly into combat and conversation. Use five linked arenas:

1. **Position** — status, jurisdiction, audience, and who may speak
2. **Information** — facts, proof, secrets, false analysis, and transmission
3. **Resources** — water, spice, transport, labor, equipment, shelter, and time
4. **Bodies** — training, poison, duels, shields, injury, and environmental exposure
5. **Meaning** — loyalty, honor, faith, fear, legitimacy, and remembered obligation

A consequence should usually move at least two arenas. Exposure of a spice theft may restore resources while destroying a patron's position; winning a duel may settle a personal claim while escalating kanly.

## Default Pressure Cycle

For a bounded opening, define:

- one visible shortage or contradiction
- one institution demanding a result
- one affected community with its own plan
- one rival actor exploiting delay
- one environmental deadline
- one piece of evidence whose custody matters
- one choice that cannot satisfy every obligation

Advance pressure through causal action rather than random betrayal.

## Scale Ladder

| Scale | What PCs directly control | What remains off-screen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Personal | body, immediate words, carried assets, one relationship | House strategy and planetary outcomes |
| Agent | mission team, local contacts, bounded operation | rulers' final policy and Imperial systems |
| Household | a department, facility, route, concession, or retinue | whole-world control |
| Architect | distant assets, negotiations, House projects, multiple fronts | automatic compliance and perfect information |
| House ruler | domains, succession, strategic alliances, war posture | freedom from Guild, CHOAM, Landsraad, ecology, or consequence |
| Imperial | regime and multi-world transformation | suitability for default play; requires a new campaign contract |

Default opening: agent scale, with one household-level asset and no architect action until the group understands its consequences.
