# Dune Session 0 Options

## Player-Safe Use

This file contains no frame solutions. It proposes decisions but accepts none. Present one unresolved decision at a time, record the player's answer in campaign-owned files, and keep unselected options as reference only.

## Recommended Default Pitch

You are a mixed field cell serving an original Minor House on a small northern-Arrakis spice concession before the events of *Dune*. Your House needs the concession, but it does not own the desert, the people who work it, or your loyalties. Begin with one settlement, one route, one water institution, and a contradiction in the production ledger. Grow only when your choices create wider consequences.

Default selections proposed, not accepted:

- Continuity: Frank Herbert core
- Era: late Corrino Imperium before the Atreides transfer; exact year unset
- Frame: `dune-frame-dry-ledger`
- Scale: agent
- Party: original Minor House field cell
- Theater: Hagga Basin fringe
- Famous characters: background-only or absent
- Rules: system-neutral unless the group selects 2d20 or another implementation
- Spoilers: first-novel broad premise only

## Decision Order

1. Continuity
2. Era and spoiler ceiling
3. Campaign fantasy and party mandate
4. Opening theater and scale
5. House or community relationship
6. Tone and thematic emphasis
7. Rules and consequence model
8. Player-authored characters and ties
9. Safety boundaries and privacy
10. Opening frame and materialization consent

Do not ask for a complete encyclopedia of choices at once.

## Continuity Choice

Choose one:

- `fh-core`: Frank Herbert's six novels; default-era facts come mainly from *Dune*
- `expanded-novels`: name which Brian Herbert/Kevin J. Anderson works may contribute
- `legendary-screen`: select the film/series presentation as visible continuity
- `prophecy-era`: remote pre-Paul play requiring a separate research pass
- `awakening-alternate`: official Paul-less alternate timeline
- `campaign-divergent`: state the exact branch point and desired consequences

Then choose a play lens separately: system-neutral, Modiphius-compatible, screen-cinematic, or custom rules.

## Era And Spoiler Ceiling

| Option | Player-safe description | Spoiler intensity |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Late Corrino, pre-transfer | House rivalry and Arrakis extraction before the first novel begins | Low |
| Atreides transition | A new ruling House enters a prepared trap on Arrakis | High for *Dune* |
| Muad'Dib ascension | Imperial collapse and Fremen-led transformation | Very high |
| Messiah or regency | Rule, religion, conspiracy, and prescient consequence | Very high |
| Later Frank Herbert era | Radically transformed institutions and human futures | Very high; targeted research required |
| *Dune: Prophecy* era | Sisterhood origins ten millennia before Paul | Separate screen/expanded continuity |
| *Dune: Awakening* | Paul was never born; survival and faction ascent on alternate Arrakis | Alternate premise disclosed by necessity |

Ask: “Which published outcomes may the GM treat as known background, and which should remain unrevealed?”

## Fourteen Player Fantasies

| Fantasy | What players do | Best scale | Onboarding | Main caution |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| 1. Great House agents | solve sensitive missions for a powerful patron | agent | 5 | patron duty must not erase personal choice |
| 2. Minor House builders | define a homeworld and raise a vulnerable House | household → architect | 4 | avoid making subjects mere House resources |
| 3. House rulers | manage domains, alliances, succession, and distant agents | architect → ruler | 2 | high complexity and broad consequence |
| 4. Spice-operations cell | protect or reform a concession under desert pressure | agent | 5 | labor and ecology cannot be background costs |
| 5. CHOAM auditors and factors | investigate contracts, quotas, stockpiles, and ownership | agent → architect | 3 | exact corporate detail needs research |
| 6. Landsraad advocates | argue petitions, mediate kanly, and build coalitions | agent → architect | 2 | procedure is not justice |
| 7. Smugglers and merchants | move people, spice, water, and secrets off ledger | personal → agent | 4 | define community ties beyond profit |
| 8. Fremen sietch members | protect and shape a specific community's future | personal → community | 3 | targeted cultural review; no monolith or outsider savior |
| 9. Arrakeen or pyon locals | survive and organize beneath House extraction | personal → community | 4 | status must not imply helplessness |
| 10. Guild-passage specialists | navigate manifests, passengers, and transport leverage | agent | 3 | preserve Guild mystery and bounded authority |
| 11. School-trained operatives | use scarce human disciplines amid divided loyalty | personal → agent | 4 | exact powers, conditioning, and allegiance require consent |
| 12. Ecologists and water stewards | investigate living systems and negotiate use | agent | 4 | knowledge must be situated, not a shortcut to mastery |
| 13. Imperial courtiers or Sardaukar | operate near throne, ritual, force, and disgrace | agent → architect | 2 | violence, authoritarian service, and canon collision |
| 14. *Awakening* survivalists | build, craft, explore, and choose factions in alternate history | personal → base | 4 | isolate continuity and define PvP/base-loss boundaries |

Recommended first campaign: combine fantasies 1, 4, 9, 11, and 12 in one mixed field cell. A character may fit more than one fantasy.

## Party Mandate Options

Choose one shared reason to remain together:

- original Minor House field cell
- concession emergency team
- CHOAM-chartered audit delegation
- Landsraad evidence and negotiation team
- smuggler crew with one protected community
- residents of one Arrakis district or village
- members of one specifically designed sietch community
- passengers jointly responsible for a Guild transit crisis
- original House council using agent and architect play
- independent alternate-timeline survival cell

The mandate grants a reason to cooperate, not a mandatory belief. Ask each player what duty they accept, what authority they distrust, and what exit remains possible.

## Original House Questions

If a House is selected, decide collaboratively:

- House scale: minor, major, declining, rising, or client
- homeworld climate and primary livelihood
- virtue the House publicly claims
- obligation it reliably honors
- injustice or contradiction it avoids naming
- export or expertise
- CHOAM and Guild dependency
- one rival and one relationship that is not rivalry
- why the Arrakis concession matters
- what ordinary residents may demand from the House
- who can challenge the ruler legitimately
- succession posture without assigning a PC's place in it

Do not select a House name, heraldry, motto, or family tie on a player's behalf.

## Opening Theater Choice

- Hagga Basin fringe: best default balance of survival, intrigue, labor, and ecology
- Arrakeen district: city investigation and mixed social status
- Deep Desert: relationship-first survival and Fremen-centered play
- Carthag: coercive extraction and hostile administration
- Original House homeworld: player investment and Minor House rise
- Kaitain: high-complexity politics and CHOAM leverage
- Guild passage: bounded locked-location crisis
- Giedi Prime: hostile diplomacy with intensive content review
- Caladan: transition, loyalty, and household roots
- Ix: technology-boundary espionage after targeted research

Select one opening theater and at most one distant connection.

## Scale Choice

- Personal: survival, loyalty, one neighborhood or work group
- Agent: one mission team with limited authority; default
- Household: one facility, office, route, concession, or retinue
- Architect: distant assets and multi-front House plans
- House ruler: domains and strategic policy
- Imperial: regime transformation; unsuitable as a default opening

Ask: “What can the party command without asking, and what remains beyond its authority?”

## Tone Dials

Rate each from 0 (background or absent) to 3 (central and explicit):

- court intrigue
- espionage and betrayal
- desert survival
- ecological consequence
- labor and class conflict
- House-building strategy
- religion and prophecy
- prescience and altered awareness
- poison and assassination
- martial conflict
- body horror
- romance and family politics
- moral compromise
- famous-character proximity

A high setting intensity does not waive player boundaries.

## Safety Topics

Discuss lines, veils, or opt-in requirements for:

- dehydration, starvation, heat injury, and environmental death
- colonial occupation, displacement, and resource extraction
- slavery, forced labor, and class violence
- torture, poison, assassination, and execution
- reproductive coercion, eugenics, arranged marriage, and pregnancy
- religious manipulation, fanaticism, holy war, and genocide
- addiction, withdrawal, and involuntary spice exposure
- body horror, identity alteration, and medical violation
- coercive Voice-like influence and loss of agency
- family betrayal and inherited obligation
- racism, cultural appropriation, and outsider-savior narratives
- PvP, secret faction loyalty, betrayal, and base destruction

No hidden PC conditioning, pregnancy, ancestry, allegiance, romance, or betrayal may be introduced without the relevant player's explicit agreement.

## Rules And Resolution Choice

Choose one:

- system-neutral narrative resolution
- *Dune: Adventures in the Imperium* 2d20, using owned rules
- another named system with a translation pass
- lightweight custom procedure designed after the fiction is locked

Then decide:

- how water and spice are tracked, if at all
- whether social and physical conflicts share stakes
- injury and recovery severity
- poison visibility and consent
- how House assets enter play
- whether architect actions are available
- how prescient information is generated and who controls interpretation
- whether equipment loss and base loss are reversible

This package supplies no copied rules or stat blocks.

## Player-Authored Character Prompts

Each player may answer, decline, or defer:

- What is your name, identity, appearance, and home?
- Which community claims you, and how do you answer that claim?
- What training or work makes you useful to the party?
- Who taught you, and what did you reject from that teaching?
- What do you believe about House rule, water, spice, faith, and obligation?
- Which loyalty is chosen rather than inherited?
- What debt, oath, ambition, grief, secret, or hope do you want in play?
- What is your relationship to melange exposure?
- What authority may the party exercise over you, if any?
- Which faction may court you but not own you?
- What tie do you want with another PC?
- What personal topic remains private even from other players?

Do not convert an unanswered prompt into a fact.

## Role And Training Options

Offer as fiction-first possibilities, not required classes:

- envoy or courtier
- strategist or Mentat-trained analyst
- Bene Gesserit-trained character with player-defined institutional relationship
- Suk-trained or local medical practitioner
- Swordmaster, duelist, guard, or soldier
- spy, courier, or security specialist
- harvester, carryall, ornithopter, or maintenance specialist
- water steward, ecologist, weather observer, or surveyor
- merchant, CHOAM functionary, accountant, or contract advocate
- smuggler, guide, salvager, or broker
- worker organizer, local official, artisan, or religious figure
- player-authored Fremen community role after cultural design

Training suggests capability; it does not predetermine personality or loyalty.

## Knowledge And Spoiler Choice

Decide separately:

- what players know as Dune fans
- what their characters know through position and experience
- what later-novel outcomes are off limits
- whether character ignorance may coexist with player knowledge
- how prescient visions are shared
- which faction secrets a PC may propose for their own backstory
- whether false beliefs are welcome and who controls them

Use [Knowledge Layers](knowledge_layers.md) after acceptance.
