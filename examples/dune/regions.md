# Dune Regions And Campaign Theaters

## Use Contract

These cards are selectable theaters, not a travel gazetteer and not active state. Materialize one opening theater plus a small connection network. Scores compare onboarding utility from 1 (low) to 5 (high); they are package-original design judgments, not canon ratings.

## Region Cards

### `dune-region-arrakeen` — Northern Arrakis And Arrakeen

- Status: template theater; local district is campaign-instance
- Promise: city intrigue under environmental pressure, where House administration, markets, intelligence, labor, and desert access meet
- Routine: water accounting, residency security, cargo inspection, maintenance, hospitality, permits, rumors, and heat-managed movement
- Institutions: governing House apparatus, water sellers and keepers, merchants, workers, smugglers, visiting agents, religious authorities, and off-world specialists
- Pressures: spice quota, sabotage fear, unequal water access, regime transition, hidden loyalties, and evidence that can damage a House
- Travel: Shield Wall routes, ornithopter fields, roads to industrial sites, and controlled passages toward open desert
- Affordances: investigation, social conflict, logistics, covert meetings, urban survival, and mixed-status parties
- Dangers: surveillance, poison, false credentials, heat, crowd pressure, shield/lasgun risk, and a city authority that can redefine the mission
- Player-known account: Arrakeen is an administrative and commercial center from which off-world power attempts to govern a world it does not master.
- Caution: do not make the city only a palace backdrop; create workers, neighborhoods, water institutions, and local agendas.
- Character fits: House agent, merchant, worker, Suk-trained medic, Mentat, smuggler contact, court envoy, local guide
- Expansion trigger: open another district only when a current actor, route, or consequence requires it
- Source claims: `claim-dune-arrakeen-carthag`, `claim-dune-water`, `claim-dune-house-play`, `claim-dune-class-daily-life`.

### `dune-region-hagga` — Hagga Basin Concession Fringe

- Status: default campaign-instance canvas anchored to a canonical geographic theater
- Promise: a bounded spice frontier where quota, water, equipment, weather, labor, smugglers, and Fremen interests collide
- Routine: shift changes, crawler deployment, carryall readiness, weather reports, ration seals, equipment salvage, convoy protection, and ledgers
- Institutions: an original concession office, governing-House overseer, labor community, water steward, maintenance crew, CHOAM-linked auditor, smugglers, and nearby desert actors
- Pressures: wormsign, storm windows, missing production, falsified manifests, dangerous maintenance shortcuts, and a community that cannot simply leave
- Travel: one protected settlement, one harvester zone, one contested route, one rock refuge, and one edge leading deeper into the bled
- Affordances: field operations, mystery, resource dilemmas, rescue, negotiation, and agent-to-house escalation
- Dangers: heat, thirst, sand, worms, equipment loss, concealed extraction, violence without quick reinforcement
- Player-known account: the Imperium's richest commodity is produced through dangerous routines whose failures are often borne by people far from the throne.
- Caution: never treat labor casualties or water deprivation as flavor for an efficiency puzzle.
- Character fits: mixed House field cell, harvester specialist, worker advocate, ecological observer, smuggler, security agent, water keeper
- Expansion trigger: reveal a second concession or sietch connection only after consequences exceed the local network
- Source claims: `claim-dune-arrakis-spice`, `claim-dune-water`, `claim-dune-worm-spice-cycle`, `claim-dune-play-scales`.

### `dune-region-deep-desert` — Deep Desert And Sietch Territories

- Status: protected theater; requires Fremen-centered consent and cultural review
- Promise: survival and negotiation in a living landscape where outsiders' maps, schedules, and ownership claims fail
- Routine: water discipline, stillsuit care, terrain reading, movement timing, shelter, communal obligation, and guarded ecological work
- Institutions: specific sietch communities, kin and work groups, naibs and other locally defined roles, ecological stewards, scouts, traders, and ritual authorities
- Pressures: Imperial intrusion, resource extraction, concealed routes, old obligations, internal disagreement, and the cost of revealing knowledge
- Travel: rock, erg, basin, cave, weather path, worm territory, and routes not placed on an outsider-facing map
- Affordances: survival, diplomacy, community-centered choices, ecological investigation, resistance, and transformation of loyalties
- Dangers: exposure, storm, worm, failed equipment, betrayal of location, appropriation of sacred or survival knowledge
- Player-known account: Fremen communities possess deep desert knowledge and political agency that Imperial powers systematically underestimate.
- Caution: Fremen are not a monolith, encounter reward, mystical test, or guaranteed ally. Do not make an outsider the natural leader of a selected sietch campaign.
- Character fits: player-authored Fremen members, trusted traders, defectors, debt-bearers, ecological collaborators, carefully bounded outsiders
- Expansion trigger: a community chooses to disclose another route, group, or objective; outsiders do not unlock a culture by passing one test
- Source claims: `claim-dune-fremen`, `claim-dune-water`, `claim-dune-worm-spice-cycle`, `claim-dune-religion`.

### `dune-region-carthag` — Carthag And The Extraction Apparatus

- Status: template theater in the default era; exact administration is era-sensitive
- Promise: industrial and bureaucratic intrigue where coercion, spectacle, logistics, and rival agents protect spice output
- Routine: manifests, worker discipline, security sweeps, entertainment, maintenance, informants, off-ledger trade, and administrative rivalry
- Institutions: governing-House offices, security services, industrial managers, water and transport authorities, merchants, smugglers, and coerced labor systems
- Pressures: quotas, corruption, fear, rivalry between offices, evidence suppression, and the distance between official abundance and worker precarity
- Travel: administrative compounds, industrial districts, markets, landing fields, worker quarters, and routes into the basin
- Affordances: infiltration, labor politics, extraction of a person or record, counterintelligence, and morally compromised patronage
- Dangers: arbitrary authority, torture or coercion themes, poison, surveillance, collective punishment, and false accusations
- Player-known account: Carthag can represent an extraction regime's attempt to turn Arrakis into obedient output.
- Caution: set boundaries before centering slavery, torture, or dehumanizing labor; affected people need goals beyond rescue by elites.
- Character fits: rival-House envoy, local worker, compromised official, spy, medic, merchant, smuggler, escape organizer
- Expansion trigger: follow a named chain of responsibility rather than adding anonymous cruelty
- Source claims: `claim-dune-arrakeen-carthag`, `claim-dune-class-daily-life`, `claim-dune-house-play`.

### `dune-region-kaitain` — Kaitain And The Landsraad

- Status: high-politics theater; exact court composition is campaign-instance
- Promise: ceremonial beauty and lethal consequence, where access, wording, reputation, CHOAM leverage, and kanly can decide worlds
- Routine: audiences, petitions, salons, dossiers, gifts, marriage signals, debate, private bargains, and carefully staged insult
- Institutions: Imperial court, Landsraad delegations, Great and Minor Houses, CHOAM interests, Guild representatives, Sisterhood observers, Sardaukar, and specialist advisers
- Pressures: succession, bloc formation, commercial votes, vendetta, scandal, evidence authenticity, and Imperial favor
- Travel: one court precinct, one delegation residence, one neutral meeting place, and one route controlled by another institution
- Affordances: diplomacy, legal framing, reputation conflict, blackmail, alliance building, and architect-level play
- Dangers: disgrace, hostage-like hospitality, misread ritual, deniable violence, and consequences carried home by dependents
- Player-known account: the Landsraad allows Houses to bargain and resist Imperial concentration while preserving an unequal feudal order.
- Caution: procedure is not justice; show the worlds and people affected by elegant decisions.
- Character fits: House envoy, heir, advocate, CHOAM agent, Bene Gesserit, Mentat, bodyguard, court servant, investigator
- Expansion trigger: add a House delegation only when it has a concrete stake and capacity
- Source claims: `claim-dune-power-balance`, `claim-dune-choam`, `claim-dune-kanly-convention`, `claim-dune-kaitain`.

### `dune-region-giedi-prime` — Giedi Prime

- Status: canon theater with high named-character collision; local site must be original
- Promise: a visit inside a hostile industrial House domain where hospitality, production, fear, and spectacle conceal layered agendas
- Routine: factory output, security hierarchy, patronage, surveillance, controlled entertainment, pollution exposure, and covert bargaining
- Institutions: House Harkonnen branches appropriate to the selected era, industrial managers, security forces, workers, household factions, merchants, and visiting delegations
- Pressures: ambition, punishment, rivalry inside the ruling apparatus, extraction, reputation, and visitors' dependence on hosts
- Travel: one factory district, guest compound, service route, and private venue; do not tour the whole planet by default
- Affordances: hostile diplomacy, infiltration, extraction, labor-centered resistance, and evidence gathering
- Dangers: coercion, body horror, torture themes, environmental harm, surveillance, and canon-character gravitational pull
- Player-known account: the world is associated with Harkonnen industrial power and a political culture shaped by exploitation and fear in the opening era.
- Caution: avoid physiognomic moral coding and spectacle-only suffering; villainy should arise from choices and systems.
- Character fits: envoy, infiltrator, worker contact, commercial agent, defector, hostage, bodyguard
- Expansion trigger: targeted source verification before using a named canon location or official
- Source claims: `claim-dune-feudal-imperium`, `claim-dune-class-daily-life`, `claim-dune-play-scales`.

### `dune-region-caladan` — Caladan

- Status: canon theater; default use is before the Atreides transfer
- Promise: a water-rich House world whose routines, loyalties, agriculture, sea, and military culture contrast sharply with Arrakis
- Routine: estate administration, farming and fisheries, port movement, training, household obligation, and preparation for off-world duty
- Institutions: era-appropriate Atreides household branches, local communities, military retainers, merchants, and Guild-linked transport
- Pressures: departure, reassignment, succession, loyalty to people versus House, supply preparation, and intelligence about Arrakis
- Travel: one port or estate, one local community, and one embarkation route
- Affordances: character grounding, transition missions, household politics, farewells, and consequences of noble relocation
- Dangers: turning House reputation into universal virtue, relying on named protagonists, or making local people scenery for departure
- Player-known account: Caladan's environment and governing traditions provide a deliberate contrast with the desert fief.
- Caution: an Atreides-aligned campaign still needs disagreement, limits, and people whose interests differ from the ruling House.
- Character fits: retainer, local civilian, logistics officer, envoy, displaced worker, household specialist
- Expansion trigger: use only if the campaign selects an Atreides-adjacent premise and fixes canon boundaries
- Source claims: `claim-dune-house-play`, `claim-dune-class-daily-life`, `claim-dune-core-scope`.

### `dune-region-heighliner` — Guild Passage And Heighliner

- Status: bounded transit theater
- Promise: a sealed interstellar crossing where rival passengers share Guild-controlled space and cannot easily call home
- Routine: manifests, separation of delegations, cargo custody, etiquette, waiting, inspection, and guarded movement
- Institutions: Guild personnel, House delegations, merchants, pilgrims, security details, and passengers with concealed purposes
- Pressures: schedule, undeclared cargo, jurisdiction, murder or sabotage, diplomatic immunity, and the cost of delaying the Guild
- Travel: embarkation hold, assigned vessel or quarters, neutral passage, cargo zone, and controlled exit
- Affordances: locked-location mystery, negotiation, transport politics, and consequences of limited communication
- Dangers: overdefining Guild secrets, treating the heighliner as a generic cruise ship, and ignoring faction separation or transport leverage
- Player-known account: interstellar passage is possible because the Guild controls a system on which every major power depends.
- Caution: verify exact procedures only when they become a solution; retain uncertainty around protected Guild operations.
- Character fits: House agent, merchant, courier, investigator, Guild-linked character, diplomat, guard
- Expansion trigger: arrival on one selected world; the journey does not unlock the Imperium as an unbounded map
- Source claims: `claim-dune-guild-travel`, `claim-dune-play-scales`.

### `dune-region-house-homeworld` — Original House Homeworld

- Status: campaign-instance; package supplies constraints, not facts
- Promise: maximum player investment in a home domain whose people, economy, ecology, and obligations make House ambition concrete
- Required choices: climate, core livelihood, ruling compact, one celebrated institution, one exploited group or unresolved injustice, one Guild dependency, one export, one vulnerability, and one neighboring relationship
- Institutions: created with the players; at least one must possess legitimate authority outside the ruling family
- Pressures: succession, debt, CHOAM position, rival claim, resource transition, or an Arrakis concession that can save or damage the domain
- Travel: one capital or estate, one working community, one strategic facility, and one route off-world
- Affordances: Minor House rise, architect play, homecoming consequences, and player-authored ties
- Dangers: making an entire planet culturally uniform or assuming House interests equal public interests
- Player-known account: defined only after Session 0; no placeholder name becomes truth
- Character fits: any, provided each player chooses a meaningful relationship to the House and homeworld
- Expansion trigger: player action or a faction consequence requires another region
- Source claims: `claim-dune-house-play`, `claim-dune-play-scales` plus package-original design.

### `dune-region-ix` — Ix And Advanced-Technology Interests

- Status: optional, continuity-sensitive, targeted research required
- Promise: secrecy and procurement at the boundary between innovation, prohibition, commercial advantage, and political accusation
- Routine: compartmentalized research, contracts, testing, counterintelligence, export control, and deniable intermediaries
- Institutions: selected Ixian interests, House patrons, technical specialists, spies, Guild customers, and Imperial investigators
- Pressures: whether a device violates doctrine, who owns a design, sabotage, industrial espionage, and dependence on technologies publicly distrusted
- Travel: embassy, controlled workshop, test site, and transfer route; not a complete planetary survey
- Affordances: espionage, ethics of invention, procurement, sabotage, and legal ambiguity
- Dangers: importing later- or expanded-continuity facts into the default era; turning the thinking-machine prohibition into a simple gadget ban
- Player-known account: Ix is associated with advanced technology and political suspicion, but exact capabilities depend on era and source selection.
- Caution: verify every plot-critical device and institution against the selected continuity.
- Character fits: technical envoy, spy, Mentat, procurement agent, investigator, defector
- Expansion trigger: a selected device or contract becomes central and receives a targeted provenance pass
- Source claims: `claim-dune-limits-computers`, `claim-dune-human-schools`, `claim-dune-kaitain`.

## Default Opening Network

A `dune-region-hagga` materialization should start with only:

- one original settlement
- one spice concession and harvester zone
- one contested route
- one rock refuge or weather station
- one water institution
- one governing-House office
- one affected community organization
- one smuggler or desert contact
- four to six faces
- one visible contradiction
- one hidden causal chain

Arrakeen, a sietch, Kaitain, and the House homeworld remain distant until current actors make them relevant.
