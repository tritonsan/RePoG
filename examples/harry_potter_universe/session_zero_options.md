# Harry Potter Universe Session 0 Options

## Player-Safe Use

This file contains no campaign-frame solutions. It may be shown to players after the group agrees that it wants to engage with the Harry Potter fictional setting. Source hierarchy, hidden facts, and technical materialization remain Designer/GM concerns.

## Default Pitch

It is 2001, roughly three years after the 1998 conflict and political transition, but before the seven-novel epilogue. In an original hidden British magical quarter, one public travel hearth has begun sending some people to the wrong destinations and refusing others entirely. Patients miss referrals, shops lose deliveries, and residents with incomplete wartime records are told that their homes do not exist. You are three to five original people connected to this community. You might be a recent graduate, healer, trader, clerk, journalist, Squib organizer, route worker, creature caretaker, or another locally grounded practitioner. Your task is not to replace famous heroes. It is to decide whom repair serves and what a safer magical society should remember.

The year, quarter, characters, route failure, and recovery conditions are original campaign proposals. Select another era or fantasy if this pitch does not fit.

## Decisions In Order

### 1. Property Engagement

Choose one:

- use the official Harry Potter fictional universe with this package's source boundaries
- use a generic wizard-school or hidden-magic world instead
- use a heavily divergent setting that retains only selected motifs
- do not use this property

Real-world views about creators or rights-holders are not in-fiction debate prompts. Nobody must justify declining the setting.

### 2. Continuity

- `harry-potter-book-core` — seven novels plus compatible official setting writings; default
- `harry-potter-screen-hp` — Harry Potter film adaptation continuity
- `harry-potter-screen-fantastic-beasts` — Fantastic Beasts screen continuity
- `harry-potter-stage-cursed-child` — opt-in stage continuation
- `harry-potter-game-hogwarts-legacy` — opt-in nineteenth-century game continuity
- `harry-potter-campaign-divergent` — record a precise divergence

Select explicit crossover rules if more than one is allowed. Familiar visual design does not silently merge continuities.

### 3. Era And Spoiler Ceiling

| Era | Campaign promise | Minimum suggested spoiler band |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `harry-potter-era-secrecy-foundation` | institutions adjusting to concealed life | `S1`, with targeted historical research |
| `harry-potter-era-1800s` | historical school, Ministry, transport, or social change | `S1`; `S6` only for Legacy plot |
| `harry-potter-era-fantastic-beasts` | dates established by the selected Fantastic Beasts film/script; broader 1920s–1940s only with separate sources | `S4` if selected-work outcomes matter |
| `harry-potter-era-first-war` | resistance, family survival, and strained institutions | `S3` |
| `harry-potter-era-interwar` | recovery, denial, and inherited memory | `S2`–`S3` |
| `harry-potter-era-main-series` | school or community life during 1991–1998 | `S1`–`S3` by selected year |
| `harry-potter-era-postwar` | repair, accountability, and ordinary magical life | `S3`; package default |
| `harry-potter-era-stage-future` | next-generation consequences | `S5` |

Set exact year, season, known published events, and whether outcomes may be changed. For post-war play, also choose an epilogue-future policy: preserve all established epilogue facts, preserve only explicitly selected facts, or record a divergence. The package default preserves the seven-novel epilogue while loading only facts relevant to current decisions; Cursed Child remains separately opt-in.

### 4. Rules And Magic Posture

Choose system-neutral narration or name an owned game system. Then decide:

- strict source-listed magic, bounded original magic, or highly divergent magic
- how new spells, potions, objects, and creatures are approved
- whether wands are expected, optional, inaccessible, or character-specific
- how failure, counters, evidence, injury, healing, and secrecy work
- whether combat is rare, moderate, or central
- whether rules abstractions are visible only to players or also fictional facts

No video-game interface is assumed.

### 5. Theater And Scale

Choose one physical theater from [Regions](regions.md) and at most one connection layer. Default: `harry-potter-region-recovery-quarter` plus optional `harry-potter-region-floo-layer`.

Choose scale:

- one room, ward, shop, or club
- one street, village edge, school year, department, or service
- one route or regional network
- national institution
- international circuit

The package recommends the second option. Larger scales require more research and stronger knowledge boundaries.

### 6. Group Fantasy And Mandate

Choose one row from the comparison below or combine at most two compatible rows. Define why the group cooperates, what it can refuse, who depends on it, and how a member can leave safely.

### 7. Famous-Character Proximity

- none; published people remain history or public names — default
- distant; one may be an office-holder, author, or reported figure
- bounded contact; at most two appear for narrow reasons
- high-canon; PCs interact with published figures and outcomes after specific sourcing
- divergent; published roles may change after a recorded divergence

A famous relative, mentor, enemy, or romantic tie is never assigned without player request.

### 8. Social Themes

For each topic choose foreground, background, transformed, or excluded:

- blood-status supremacy and Muggle-born discrimination
- Squib access and family exclusion
- werewolf stigma, registration, and transformation risk
- house-elf bondage and freedom
- goblin-wizard ownership and political conflict
- prison conditions and coercive law
- memory alteration and consent
- school discipline and adult authority
- war trauma, bereavement, and political violence
- non-magical witnesses and secrecy enforcement

If foregrounded, identify affected people's agency and desired play focus. Do not use oppression merely to darken scenery.

### 9. Tone And Consequence

Choose two primary tones and one excluded tone:

- wonder and discovery
- cozy community responsibility
- school friendship
- procedural mystery
- medical or care drama
- political reform
- journalistic pressure
- magical adventure
- wartime resistance
- gothic danger
- sporting competition
- family legacy

Set consequence levels for injury, death, expulsion, imprisonment, reputational harm, loss of magic, memory alteration, and harm to creatures.

### 10. Safety And Authorship

Record lines, veils, pause/rewind tools, check-in method, and private communication route. Student campaigns must define age band, adult safeguarding, romance boundaries, and excluded content involving minors.

## Visible Frame Menu

- **The Mended Network:** restore a failing community travel hearth without erasing the people missing from official records.
- **The Fifth Table:** make a shared school room safe and accessible when rules and records close it around the wrong students.
- **The Quiet Ward:** protect patients while tracing an inconsistent support draught through a pressured care system.
- **Ink Before Dawn:** verify and publish a dangerous Ministry leak without exposing the people whose testimony matters.
- **The Borrowed Key:** mediate competing custody histories around a crafted object needed by a public service.
- **The Lantern Delegation:** host an international exchange while protecting migrating creatures and preventing false blame.

Create a custom frame if none fits. Do not ask players to select among hidden truths.

## Accessibility And Dignity Contract

- Magic does not make accessibility unnecessary.
- A disability is not a puzzle, punishment, moral sign, or mandatory cure arc.
- Squib, Muggle-born, werewolf, goblin, house-elf, and other fictional categories do not map automatically onto real identities.
- Blood-status rhetoric is treated as ideology and harm, never neutral biological truth.
- House-elf servitude is not comic background when present.
- Goblin characters are people with varied interests, not a shorthand for finance, greed, or distrust.
- Werewolf characters control private disclosure outside immediate agreed safety needs.
- Coercive spells, truth effects, love potions, memory changes, possession, and mind access require consent and consequence rules.
- Pronouns, names, gender, and bodies are not invalidated by transformation or detection magic.
- A participant may veto a metaphor or theme without defending the veto.

## Group Bond Prompts

Choose two or three:

- We maintain a service that none of us can protect alone.
- One community trusted us when an institution would not.
- We share custody of evidence that could help and endanger different people.
- We failed someone during a prior incident and agreed on a repair.
- Our professions overlap at one hidden threshold.
- We disagree about an institution but share responsibility for its users.
- A person each of us trusts asked us to cooperate, then stepped away.
- We built a mutual-aid practice with a clear refusal and exit agreement.
