## What This Package Is

This is a researched setting package for the fictional universe established by the seven Harry Potter novels and extended through official Wizarding World material. It is not a generic magic-school kit, a Hogwarts student simulator, a retelling of Harry Potter's story, a film-production guide, a Fantastic Beasts campaign, a stage adaptation, or a Hogwarts Legacy game conversion.

The public-facing Wizarding World brand includes books, films, stage work, games, experiences, and editorial material. Brand inclusion does not make every presentation detail one interchangeable continuity. This package therefore distinguishes:

- the seven-novel narrative and setting core
- official setting writings and encyclopedia summaries
- the Harry Potter film adaptations
- the Fantastic Beasts screen continuity and the dates/events established by each selected work
- the post-epilogue stage continuation of *Harry Potter and the Cursed Child*
- the nineteenth-century interactive continuity of *Hogwarts Legacy*
- package-original or campaign-divergent material

A future campaign selects one continuity policy and one era before importing history, institutions, named people, visual design, spell behavior, or technology. Source claims: `claim-harry-potter-universe-scope`, `claim-harry-potter-seven-books`, `claim-harry-potter-era-chronology`, `claim-harry-potter-no-universal-canon-policy`.

## Player Promise

You play original people living within a concealed magical society: learning, healing, trading, investigating, reporting, caring for creatures, maintaining hidden routes, reforming institutions, protecting neighbors, or deciding which inherited customs deserve to survive. Magic expands what people can attempt, but it does not erase law, labor, prejudice, consent, evidence, scarcity, injury, or consequence.

The default opening is a bounded post-war story set before the seven-novel epilogue. In 2001, one original British magical quarter depends on a fragile transport-and-service network. Misrouted journeys are separating residents from work, treatment, supplies, and family. A three-to-five-person circle with different relationships to magic and institutions must discover what the network is protecting, who is being excluded, and what a fair repair would require. Hogwarts and famous protagonists are not required.

The year, locale, recovery pressure, network behavior, active faces, and hidden cause are RePoG-original proposals, not official chronology claims.

## Selection Locks

Nothing below is active merely because this package exists. Session 0 must affirmatively accept the package, continuity, era, group fantasy, theater, source policy, and relevant safeguards. After acceptance, the selected fictional locks apply until the table records a change; unselected proposals remain inert.

- The setting is the Harry Potter fictional universe, not a generic wizard-school genre world.
- The seven novels form the primary narrative and setting spine; official web writings supplement rather than silently overwrite them.
- One era and one media-continuity policy govern the opening.
- The default is 2001 post-war Britain, but exact reconstruction conditions are campaign-instance content.
- Hogwarts is a selectable theater, not the compulsory center of every life or campaign.
- The default group contains three to five original characters with a practical shared responsibility and a meaningful right to refuse.
- Published protagonists, villains, teachers, and office-holders remain background, history, rumor, or absent unless proximity is accepted.
- Film production design, stage effects, game maps, combat loops, inventory limits, cooldowns, talent trees, quest markers, and collectible density are not automatic setting physics.
- Magic requires an established method, capability, access path, cost or risk, target, and limit.
- Blood-status language describes an in-world ideology and prejudice; it never establishes real worth, morality, capability, or player eligibility.
- A Hogwarts House is not a deterministic personality diagnosis. Assignment, affiliation, and importance remain player-consented.
- Player identity, pronouns, culture, body, disability, magical status, family, House, wand, familiar, history, relationships, trauma, loyalties, and boundaries remain player-authored.
- House-elf bondage, goblin-wizard conflict, werewolf stigma, Squib exclusion, imprisonment, coercive magic, and school power imbalances are opt-in themes with explicit handling agreements.
- Accepted campaign history outranks later package updates.

## Four Semantic Layers

### Template-Fixed

Selected fictional invariants include the accepted continuity, magical secrecy, bounded methods of magic, fallible institutions, uneven access, nonhuman agency, ordinary people's causal importance, and separation of fiction from media mechanics. They may change only through an explicit campaign divergence. Process safeguards—affirmative selection, exclusive player authority over authored/private fields, GM/player knowledge separation, consent, and safety boundaries—cannot be changed by a fictional divergence; only the affected participant may grant narrow, explicit permission.

### Campaign-Instance

Exact year, season, locale, institution, school status, group mandate, active services, route map, local economy, magical capabilities, laws in force, named-character proximity, factions, faces, clocks, hidden truth, rules implementation, and consequences.

### Player-Authored

Name, pronouns, identity, culture, language, body, disability, magical status, family and ancestry, relationship to blood-status labels, school and House history, wand or non-wand practice, profession, companions, beliefs, ties, private knowledge, fears, ambitions, trauma, and boundaries.

### Era/Media-Specific

Nineteenth-century game material; selected-work Fantastic Beasts screen material and separately sourced twentieth-century bridges; 1968–1981 conflict; 1991–1998 novel events; a 2001 opening before the seven-novel epilogue; later post-war reforms; 2017+ stage continuity; adaptation-only architecture, costume, performance, and spell presentation; and every gameplay abstraction.

## Default Selection Rationale

The package compares sixteen player fantasies in [Session 0 Options](session_zero_options.md). The post-war community circle is the recommended onboarding default because it:

- supports school graduates, adult practitioners, Squibs, and source-consented nonhuman perspectives
- connects care, trade, transport, law, journalism, creature work, and investigation
- permits wonder without making children responsible for every institutional crisis
- creates space after the central 1998 conflict and before the epilogue without requiring published protagonists
- keeps one neighborhood and one service failure legible
- allows restorative, investigative, social, and action-oriented solutions
- makes the exact recovery situation explicitly original rather than counterfeit canon

This ranking is package-original design judgment, not an official preference.

## Compatibility Contract

This package may inform a future campaign only when all of the following are explicit:

- selected continuity and allowed media
- selected era, year range, and spoiler ceiling
- selected locale and opening scale
- accepted group fantasy, mandate, size, and refusal rights
- selected frame or custom local pressure
- player-authored character scope
- rules source and interpretation of magical capabilities
- famous-character proximity
- handling of coercive magic, imprisonment, prejudice, servitude, minors, and institutional abuse
- user approval to materialize selected content

A future materializer must prune. It must not copy every era, region, faction, frame, secret, spell, creature, school, product outcome, or famous character into active memory.

## Authority And Isolation
